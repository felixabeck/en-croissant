### plan-r1.md

# Plan: one owner for the path-authority lock and its two failure mappings (f-20260922-01)

Plan path: `tasks/plans/2026-10-07-shared-path-authority.md`. Base `ebd934cd40de47dcd5a3b6fc10cc9120ffef6ff5`.
Mode: PLAN-ONLY drain lane (`full auto`), executor `codex`.

## Goal

The poisoned-lock and not-initialized mappings of the shared `PathAuthority` mutex are written
once, proven once, and cannot be hand-written again. Every production acquisition reaches the
authority only through one owner type, whose privacy makes a direct `.lock()` outside its
module a compile error.

## MANDATE

Verbatim, `tasks/findings.md` `f-20260922-01` (body and its 2026-09-22 annotation):

* **Where:** `src-tauri/src/main.rs` — every command that locks `state.pgn_path_authority`, roughly twenty of them, e.g. `issue_download_destination_blocking` (`:1185-1196`), `reconcile_startup_path_owners` (`:868-889`), `reconcile_engine_attachments` (`:893-925`).
* **Defect:** each of those commands maps a poisoned `Mutex` to `Error::Conflict("path authority lock was poisoned")` and an absent authority to `Error::Conflict("path authority is not initialized")`, and **no test drives either branch on any of them**. The two arms are copied by hand into every command body, so a wrong or missing mapping in one of them is invisible: the renderer would receive a differently categorised error, or a panic would propagate, and every existing gate stays green. `cargo-mutants` reports 0 backend survivors today, which means these arms are not even reached as mutable code paths worth reporting — they are simply never executed.
* **Why it matters:** `.claude/rules/async-resource-invariants.md` requires an error path and a terminal state on every asynchronous operation that reaches the process from a renderer call, and requires typed errors returned from commands and mapped at the facade. That contract is written twenty times and proven zero times. A poisoned authority mutex is exactly the state that arises after a panic inside a held guard, i.e. when the application is already damaged and the renderer's error text is the only diagnostic Felix gets.
* **Open question:** what shape does the fault-injection seam take? Driving a poisoned lock needs a deliberate panic inside a held guard, or a test-only constructor that hands out a pre-poisoned `Mutex`, or a trait seam in front of the lock. `scripts/check-rust-release-surface.mjs` refuses a public fault-injection item or import outside a `#[cfg(test)]` region (R2 of that checker, `d-20260831-07`), so the seam has to be `cfg`-gated, and `AppState` is constructed in `main.rs`'s setup closure rather than by a testable factory. Whether the answer is one gated helper on `AppState`, a `PathAuthorityHandle` newtype that owns the lock and the two mappings in one place (which would also delete twenty copies of the same four lines, universal rule 11), or per-command tests through an injected state, is a design decision spanning every one of those commands.
* **Annotation 2026-09-22:** `download_destination_is_known_blocking` in `src-tauri/src/main.rs` is the **21st** hand-written copy of the two mappings (25 occurrences of each literal in that file today). `review-minimalism` reached this finding independently […] asking for a shared authority accessor so the change "does not add another divergent copy". […] the 25 sites are **not** uniform, which is why this is a design question and not a mechanical extraction. Some take the guard inside a `run_accepted_blocking` closure with `as_mut()`, some at function top level under different binding names, at least one chains `as_mut().ok_or_else(…)?` without binding at all, and the new one takes `as_ref()` because its predicate is `&self`. A guard-returning helper would leave every caller doing `.as_mut()` again — reintroducing an `unwrap`/`expect` that `.claude/rules/async-resource-invariants.md` forbids on a renderer-driven path — so the seam has to be chosen (closure-taking helper, newtype, or injected state), which is exactly this finding's open question. Whoever takes it gets both halves at once: the seam that makes the two arms testable is the seam that removes the copies.

## Threat model and non-goals

Frozen in round 1.

* **Accidental only.** The authority becomes unavailable in two ways. A panic inside a held guard poisons the mutex. A command runs against an `AppState` whose authority was never installed (hand-built or default state, or a renderer call that races setup). No adversary is modelled. The renderer cannot choose either state.
* **Environments:** Linux (atlas, CI), Windows (CI and `pnpm rust:windows:check`), macOS (CI `rust-macos-test`). The only platform-gated acquisition is macOS-only.
* **Non-goals:** changing the renderer-visible text, category or `rootFailure` of either error; recovering from poisoning (`into_inner`) or switching to a non-poisoning mutex; changing what any site does *with* the authority, how long it holds it, or the order in which it takes other locks.

## Approach

### O1 — `SharedPathAuthority` owns the lock

A new module `src-tauri/src/infra/path_authority/shared.rs` (declared from `infra/path_authority/mod.rs`) defines `SharedPathAuthority`. It is `Clone` and wraps one private `Arc<std::sync::Mutex<Option<PathAuthority>>>`. `AppState.pgn_path_authority` takes this type, and so does every production field and parameter that today holds `Mutex<Option<PathAuthority>>`, either borrowed or in an `Arc`. A borrow becomes `&SharedPathAuthority`, and an `Arc` moved into a closure becomes a clone.

No item outside `shared.rs` can name or lock the inner mutex. That privacy is the regression anchor against a future hand-written mapping (MANDATE: "a wrong or missing mapping in one of them is invisible"). A new site that bypasses the owner does not compile.

Production surface:

* `uninitialized()` — used by `AppState::try_new`.
* `install(PathAuthority)` — used by setup. On poison it fails, and setup still fails startup as it does today.
* `with_mut(f)` — acquires the lock and checks `Some`. It then runs `f(&mut PathAuthority)` and returns `f`'s result, releasing the guard when `f` returns. If the lock is poisoned or the authority uninitialized, it returns the typed failure from O2 and **never invokes `f`**.
* `with_mut_cancellable(token, f)` — has the same contract while waiting through `lock_std_cancellable`'s polling. A cancellation observed while waiting returns `Error::Cancellation`, which stays distinguishable from both unavailability conditions.

There is deliberately no guard-returning method. Per the MANDATE annotation, a guard-returning helper leaves every caller re-checking `Option`.

### O2 — one typed unavailability and one mapping

`shared.rs` defines `AuthorityUnavailable { Poisoned, Uninitialized }`, which `with_mut`/`install` return as their error. One `From<AuthorityUnavailable> for Error` is the only place in production code where either literal appears. It maps:

* `Poisoned` to `Error::Conflict("path authority lock was poisoned")`
* `Uninitialized` to `Error::Conflict("path authority is not initialized")`

The renderer-visible payload therefore stays byte-identical: message `Conflict: path authority lock was poisoned` / `Conflict: path authority is not initialized`, category `conflict`, `rootFailure` absent. Ordinary sites propagate with `?`.

### O3 — every acquisition routes through the owner, with today's site semantics

All 54 acquisitions in the Traced premises use O1. The partial helpers `engine/process.rs::with_path_authority` and `file_workspace.rs::authority` are deleted, and their callers use O1 directly (universal rule 11). The domain helpers keep their names and contracts and call O1 inside. Site semantics that must survive unchanged:

* **Default sites** return the O2-mapped `Conflict` for either condition, as today.
* **`fs.rs` `cleanup_download_reservation_best_effort`** stays best-effort. It logs `stage` `lock` for `Poisoned` and `initialization` for `Uninitialized` with category `conflict`, and returns without propagating.
* **`main.rs` `issue_engine_image_blocking` post-install acquisition.** Either unavailability removes the installed orphan through `engine_image_error_after_cleanup` with no authority (`None`) and returns the O2-mapped error. A registration failure inside the access still passes the authority (`Some`). Acquisition failure must therefore stay distinguishable from closure failure at this site. The O1 contract (typed failure, `f`'s own result untouched) provides that.
* **`infra/path_authority/mod.rs` `probe_listing_root_failure`** uses `with_mut_cancellable`. Cancellation still returns `Error::Cancellation`. Poison and uninitialized still return `Ok(())`, so the original listing error is preserved unlabelled.
* **Setup (`main.rs:2365`)** uses `install`, and poison still fails setup.
* **Shutdown paths** (`shutdown_engine_launch_root` on macOS, `shutdown_engine_attachments`, `seal_and_drain_engine_image_issuances`) keep their `Result` contracts.
* **Lock scope and order unchanged.** Each closure covers exactly the span the guard covers today, never more. The engine-image read stays outside any authority access (`f-20260903-05`). No closure calls `BLOCKING_GATEWAY` (`.claude/rules/async-resource-invariants.md` DO). No closure takes a lock that the same site does not already take under the guard today.

### O4 — the test seam is `#[cfg(test)]` on the owner

`shared.rs` provides the following, all `#[cfg(test)] pub(crate)` (`d-20260831-07`; names avoid R2's patterns anyway):

* a constructor holding an installed authority;
* `poison()` — panics inside a held guard on a scoped thread, which is real poisoning, not a simulated one;
* `uninstall()` — sets the authority to `None`.

Every test that today builds `Mutex::new(Some(…))`, poisons by a hand-rolled thread panic, or assigns `None` uses these helpers (rule 11; seven hand-rolled copies listed in Traced premises). Tests that need to inspect the authority use `with_mut` with an explicit test `unwrap`. No production item is added for tests.

### O5 — verification that fails on revert

* **Owner unit tests (`shared.rs`).** These run against a real poisoned mutex, an uninitialized one and an installed one:
  * `with_mut` returns `Poisoned` / `Uninitialized` and the closure is provably not invoked (a flag stays unset).
  * On an installed authority the closure's own `Err` comes back untouched, not as an unavailability.
  * The `From` mapping produces the exact Display text, category `conflict` and `root_failure() == None` for both variants.
  * `with_mut_cancellable` returns `Cancellation` for a cancelled token while another thread holds the lock, and returns the typed unavailability for the poisoned and uninitialized cases.

  Revert anchor: swapping the two literals, mapping to another variant, or invoking `f` on failure each reddens a named assertion.
* **Command boundary.** At least one renderer command dispatched through `run_accepted_blocking` and one dispatched through `BLOCKING_GATEWAY` are invoked through their production command bodies with a poisoned and with an uninstalled `AppState` authority. ## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Newtype rather than an extension trait on the raw mutex.** A trait would delete the copies but leave `.lock()` reachable, so the next hand copy would again be invisible. That is the MANDATE defect. Privacy makes it a compile error at the cost of touching ~250 test lines mechanically.
* **Closure-scoped access only, no guard.** This follows the MANDATE annotation's own analysis: a guard re-exposes `Option`.
* **Typed `AuthorityUnavailable` plus one `From`, rather than returning `Error` directly.** Two sites (download cleanup log stage, engine-image orphan cleanup) must distinguish *which* failure occurred, or *that* the failure was acquisition rather than the work. A typed error lets them without parsing message text. Every other site pays one `?`.
* **Message texts unchanged.** The renderer matches the category only, and a text change would be a visible change outside MANDATE.
* **One implementation phase.** The field type change breaks all 54 sites and their tests at once, so no intermediate state compiles. Area cohesion is one concept: the authority owner.

## Threat model and non-goals

Frozen in round 1.

* **Accidental only.** The authority becomes unavailable in two ways. A panic inside a held guard poisons the mutex. A command runs against an `AppState` whose authority was never installed (hand-built or default state, or a renderer call that races setup). No adversary is modelled. The renderer cannot choose either state.
* **Environments:** Linux (atlas, CI), Windows (CI and `pnpm rust:windows:check`), macOS (CI `rust-macos-test`). The only platform-gated acquisition is macOS-only.
* **Non-goals:** changing the renderer-visible text, category or `rootFailure` of either error; recovering from poisoning (`into_inner`) or switching to a non-poisoning mutex; changing what any site does *with* the authority, how long it holds it, or the order in which it takes other locks.

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Newtype rather than an extension trait on the raw mutex.** A trait would delete the copies but leave `.lock()` reachable, so the next hand copy would again be invisible. That is the MANDATE defect. Privacy makes it a compile error at the cost of touching ~250 test lines mechanically.
* **Closure-scoped access only, no guard.** This follows the MANDATE annotation's own analysis: a guard re-exposes `Option`.
* **Typed `AuthorityUnavailable` plus one `From`, rather than returning `Error` directly.** Two sites (download cleanup log stage, engine-image orphan cleanup) must distinguish *which* failure occurred, or *that* the failure was acquisition rather than the work. A typed error lets them without parsing message text. Every other site pays one `?`.
* **Message texts unchanged.** The renderer matches the category only, and a text change would be a visible change outside MANDATE.
* **One implementation phase.** The field type change breaks all 54 sites and their tests at once, so no intermediate state compiles. Area cohesion is one concept: the authority owner.

## Approach

### O1 — `SharedPathAuthority` owns the lock

A new module `src-tauri/src/infra/path_authority/shared.rs` (declared from `infra/path_authority/mod.rs`) defines `SharedPathAuthority`. It is `Clone` and wraps one private `Arc<std::sync::Mutex<Option<PathAuthority>>>`. `AppState.pgn_path_authority` takes this type, and so does every production field and parameter that today holds `Mutex<Option<PathAuthority>>`, either borrowed or in an `Arc`. A borrow becomes `&SharedPathAuthority`, and an `Arc` moved into a closure becomes a clone.

No production item outside `shared.rs` can name or lock the inner mutex (the only other access, O4's `raw_for_test()`, exists only under `cfg(test)`). That privacy is the regression anchor against a future hand-written mapping (MANDATE: "a wrong or missing mapping in one of them is invisible"). A new site that bypasses the owner does not compile.

Production surface:

* `uninitialized()` — used by `AppState::try_new`.
* `install(PathAuthority)` — used by setup. On poison it fails, and setup still fails startup as it does today.
* `with_mut(f)` — acquires the lock and checks `Some`. It then runs `f(&mut PathAuthority)` and returns `f`'s result, releasing the guard when `f` returns. If the lock is poisoned or the authority uninitialized, it returns the typed failure from O2 and **never invokes `f`**.
* `with_mut_cancellable(token, f)` — has the same contract while waiting through `lock_std_cancellable`'s polling. A cancellation observed while waiting returns `Error::Cancellation`, which stays distinguishable from both unavailability conditions.

There is deliberately no guard-returning method. Per the MANDATE annotation, a guard-returning helper leaves every caller re-checking `Option`.

### O2 — one typed unavailability and one mapping

`shared.rs` defines `AuthorityUnavailable { Poisoned, Uninitialized }`, which `with_mut`/`install` return as their error. One `From<AuthorityUnavailable> for Error` is the only place in production code where either literal appears. It maps:

* `Poisoned` to `Error::Conflict("path authority lock was poisoned")`
* `Uninitialized` to `Error::Conflict("path authority is not initialized")`

The renderer-visible payload therefore stays byte-identical: message `Conflict: path authority lock was poisoned` / `Conflict: path authority is not initialized`, category `conflict`, `rootFailure` absent. Ordinary sites propagate with `?`.

### O3 — every acquisition routes through the owner, with today's site semantics

All 54 acquisitions in the Traced premises use O1. The partial helpers `engine/process.rs::with_path_authority` and `file_workspace.rs::authority` are deleted, and their callers use O1 directly (universal rule 11). The domain helpers keep their names and contracts and call O1 inside. Site semantics that must survive unchanged:

* **Default sites** return the O2-mapped `Conflict` for either condition, as today.
* **`fs.rs` `cleanup_download_reservation_best_effort`** stays best-effort. It logs `stage` `lock` for `Poisoned` and `initialization` for `Uninitialized` with category `conflict`, and returns without propagating.
* **`main.rs` `issue_engine_image_blocking` post-install acquisition.** Either unavailability removes the installed orphan through `engine_image_error_after_cleanup` with no authority (`None`) and returns the O2-mapped error. A registration failure inside the access still passes the authority (`Some`). Acquisition failure must therefore stay distinguishable from closure failure at this site. The O1 contract (typed failure, `f`'s own result untouched) provides that.
* **`infra/path_authority/mod.rs` `probe_listing_root_failure`** uses `with_mut_cancellable`. Cancellation still returns `Error::Cancellation`. Poison and uninitialized still return `Ok(())`, so the original listing error is preserved unlabelled.

### O4 — the test seam is `#[cfg(test)]` on the owner

`shared.rs` provides the following, all `#[cfg(test)] pub(crate)` (`d-20260831-07`; names avoid R2's patterns anyway):

* a constructor holding an installed authority;
* `poison()` — panics inside a held guard on a scoped thread, which is real poisoning, not a simulated one;
* `uninstall()` — sets the authority to `None`;
* `raw_for_test()` — returns the inner `std::sync::Mutex` for tests whose subject is the lock itself.

Every test that today builds `Mutex::new(Some(…))`, poisons by a hand-rolled thread panic, or assigns `None` uses the first three helpers (rule 11; seven hand-rolled copies listed in Traced premises).

Tests whose subject is the lock keep their proofs unchanged through `raw_for_test()`:

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Newtype rather than an extension trait on the raw mutex.** A trait would delete the copies but leave `.lock()` reachable, so the next hand copy would again be invisible. That is the MANDATE defect. Privacy makes it a compile error at the cost of touching ~250 test lines mechanically.
* **Closure-scoped access only, no guard.** This follows the MANDATE annotation's own analysis: a guard re-exposes `Option`.
* **Typed `AuthorityUnavailable` plus one `From`, rather than returning `Error` directly.** Two sites (download cleanup log stage, engine-image orphan cleanup) must distinguish *which* failure occurred, or *that* the failure was acquisition rather than the work. A typed error lets them without parsing message text. Every other site pays one `?`.
* **Message texts unchanged.** The renderer matches the category only, and a text change would be a visible change outside MANDATE.
* **One implementation phase.** The field type change breaks all 54 sites and their tests at once, so no intermediate state compiles. Area cohesion is one concept: the authority owner.
