## Mandate (fixed)

Answer and implement f-20260919-06. The question was whether to remove the in-process mapping gate
(`SearchCache::begin_preferred_replace` / `lease_preferred_mapping`, `d-20260918-17`), now that
`d-20260919-10` measured Windows POSIX-semantics replace and unlink committing under a live mapping,
or to keep it for a property other than 1224, such as bounding how long a superseded generation
stays pinned. The tests `..._external_mapper_keeps_its_generation_across_one_replace` /
`..._across_unlink` are the measurements to cite.

## Planned answer

- The gate is removed (O1). Generation and deletion never wait on readers, and readers keep the
  generation they mapped on every platform.
- Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache immediately before their single mutation
  (the pre-`8fc057bf` shape), and deletion also invalidates after it on every exit path (O2).
- O3 keeps the gate's one non-1224 property without waits: no cached mapping outlives an
  invalidation. A process-wide invalidation counter is read before each loader attempt's
  database identity probe. `insert_index` compares it inside the `indexes` critical section and
  returns the mapping uncached if it moved, and `invalidate_database` increments it before its
  eviction. If identity construction fails after the map and the counter moved, the loader returns
  the typed `Conflict("search index changed while loading")`; tombstone ordering (P14) makes every
  in-process delete race end typed.
- The external-mapper measurements stay (renamed), and the unlink one gains a same-leaf re-create
  under the still-mapped unlinked generation. The linked-ancestor and junction fixtures keep their
  index alive across the production delete. The obsolete gate tests are deleted, and the source
  scans are rewritten, including O3's critical-section scan (O4).
- `invalidate_entries` folds into `invalidate_database`, and the R5 pin moves with it. The
  decision superseding `d-20260918-17`'s mechanism is recorded by the adopting session (O5).
- One phase, sensitive role. Windows and macOS runtime proof is `rust-windows-test` /
  `rust-macos-test` on the pushed head.

## Decided autonomously (to be recorded by the adopting session)


1. **Question:** Is the in-process search-index mapping gate (d-20260918-17) kept now that Windows
   replaces and unlinks a mapped sidecar? **Chosen:** remove it. Readers keep the generation they
   mapped on every platform. Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache before and after
   their single mutation. A process-wide invalidation counter makes `insert_index` skip caching a
   mapping opened before any invalidation that began after its snapshot. A cache-identity
   construction failure after the map, with a moved counter, is returned as the loader's typed
   `Conflict("search index changed while loading")`. **Rejected:** keep the gate
   for disk-pin bounding (it blocks readers for a whole generation and writers for every search, to
   bound a pin that only an in-flight search or a late insert creates); remove it with no
   replacement (a late insert would pin a superseded or deleted generation until LRU, the next
   invalidation or exit — worse than today, P8); a per-path epoch registry (unbounded registry).
   **Reason:** `d-20260919-10`'s measurements remove the premise. The counter preserves the one
   non-1224 property without waits. Governs f-20260919-06; supersedes d-20260918-17's mechanism.
   **Reversal:** evidence of a supported volume or Windows build where an in-process mapping makes
   the POSIX-semantics replace or unlink fail.

# Plan: retire the in-process search-index mapping gate (f-20260919-06)

## Goal

Answer f-20260919-06's open question and implement the answer. The gate's stated reason (Windows
refusing to replace or unlink a mapped `.ecsi` sidecar, 1224) has been measured false. Its blocking
protocol is removed. The one property it provided that is not about 1224 is kept, without waiting:
**no cached mapping outlives a search-cache invalidation of its database.** Readers keep the
generation they mapped, on every platform, as on unix.

## MANDATE

Verbatim from `tasks/findings.md` (f-20260919-06):

> * **Defect:** `d-20260918-17` built the mapping gate on the premise that Windows refuses to replace or delete a sidecar with a live section (`ERROR_USER_MAPPED_FILE`). The replace is a `FILE_RENAME_POSIX_SEMANTICS` rename and the delete a `FILE_DISPOSITION_POSIX_SEMANTICS` unlink; `rust-windows-test` measured a durable commit under an unleased mapping on every run from 35391848926 onwards. If the delete measures the same, the gate serialises readers and writers for a failure that cannot occur.
> * **Open question:** remove the gate (readers keep the generation they mapped, as on unix), or keep it for a property other than 1224 — for example bounding how long a superseded generation stays pinned on disk by a long search? The tests `search_index_mapping_gate_external_mapper_keeps_its_generation_across_one_replace` and `..._across_unlink` are the measurements to cite.
> * **Why it matters:** the gate adds a cancellable wait to every generate and delete and a lease to every load. Harmless if unneeded, but it is concurrency machinery whose stated reason is now disproven.

The delete measurement the Defect makes conditional ("If the delete measures the same") already

Frozen at round 1.

## Approach

### O1 — The blocking gate is removed

MANDATE: "remove the gate (readers keep the generation they mapped, as on unix)"; "a cancellable
wait to every generate and delete and a lease to every load".

* Every P1/P2 gate symbol, its test hooks and the `DashMap` import that only it uses are deleted.
  `MmapSearchIndex` carries no lease, and the preferred load maps with `open_file_cancellable`.
* `generate_search_index_locked` and `delete_database_blocking` do not wait for readers. The single
  mutation (`write_entries_to_at`, `unlink_database_files`) runs exactly once, with no retry loop,
  at its current position. Deletion stays inside `delete_exclusive_cancellable`, and generation
  does not take `generation_lock` (the loader holds it).
* `SearchCache::clear` evicts the result and index caches only (it keeps `d-20260926-08`'s "never
  touches the lock registries").
* `insert_index` keeps its atomic get-or-insert under the `indexes` mutex (an existing entry for the
  identity is returned and the new mapping dropped). Displaced and evicted values are dropped after
  that mutex is released.
* `invalidate_entries`'s gate parameter and pointer eviction go. Its body folds into
  `invalidate_database`, its only remaining caller, so one function carries the canonicalize reach.
  Its `#[expect]` reason names the new site
  (`SearchCache::invalidate_database database.canonicalize`), and the R5 baseline entry is re-pinned
  to that function name and its new hash, with the method multiset unchanged (`{ canonicalize: 1 }`).
* Failure semantics: the generate and delete errors are unchanged (`MappedFile` stays classified and
  propagated for external mappers on non-POSIX volumes, P9). `Error::Cancellation` from the
  removed waits disappears; cancellation inside the row stream and the SQLite scope is unchanged.

### O2 — Pre- and post-mutation invalidation (restores the pre-gate shape)

MANDATE: removal must not lose the gate's pre-mutation eviction (P5, P6).

* Generation and deletion call `search_cache.invalidate_database(target.path())` immediately before
  their single mutation, where `begin_preferred_replace` stands today, as before `8fc057bf`.
* Deletion's post-mutation `invalidate_database` runs on **every** exit path once the
  `delete_exclusive_cancellable` closure has started the unlink, the `Err` path included (P5).
  Generation's existing post-commit invalidation on its committed and durability-uncertain paths is
  unchanged.
* Verification: `:4292` and `:4351` stay green unchanged, plus one deletion test whose unlink fails
  after the sidecar was removed (an existing fault hook, e.g. `UNLINK_SIDECAR_AFTER_IDENTITY_PROBE_HOOK`
  or the primary-removal fault) and which asserts that a mapping inserted between the pre- and
  post-mutation invalidations is not cached afterwards.

### O3 — No cached mapping outlives an invalidation (non-blocking)

MANDATE: "or keep it for a property other than 1224 — for example bounding how long a superseded
generation stays pinned on disk". This is the one such property the gate actually provided (P8).

* `SearchCache` holds a monotonically increasing invalidation counter: one process-wide atomic, no
  per-path registry, so retention is constant. `invalidate_database` increments it **before** its
  `results` and `indexes` critical sections.
* The preferred loader reads the counter **before** it opens the preferred leaf, and that snapshot
  reaches `insert_index`.
* Under the `indexes` mutex, `insert_index` first returns an existing entry for the identity
  (unchanged dedup). Otherwise, if the counter differs from the snapshot, it returns the freshly
  mapped index **uncached**; otherwise it inserts.
* Correctness, stated once: an invalidation's increment happens-before its `indexes` eviction. A
  reader insert whose critical section precedes that eviction is evicted by it; one that follows it
  sees the changed counter and is skipped. So after any `invalidate_database` returns, the cache
  holds no mapping opened before that invalidation began. An in-flight search still holds its own
  clone until it returns (cancellable), exactly as on unix. That is the accepted bound: "readers
  keep the generation they mapped".
* An invalidation of another database also makes a concurrent load skip caching. The cost is a
  re-map on that database's next search, never a wrong result (P13: invalidations are rare).
* Verification: a deterministic test in `main.rs`'s test module or `search_index.rs`. Snapshot → an
  `invalidate_database` of the same database → insert: the returned index is usable and
  `cached_index_count() == 0`. Control: with no invalidation between snapshot and insert, the index
  is cached. A second control: an invalidation of a *different* database also skips the insert,
  documenting the global counter.

* `search_index_mapping_gate_generation_lock_does_not_block_a_cache_miss_map` survives under a

* Obsolete and deleted (probe-2): the nine runtime gate tests at `search_index.rs:2106`, `:2145`,

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Remove versus keep:** removed. The gate's reason is disproven on every POSIX-capable volume
  (P9). On the other volumes nothing mutates at all. Its cost is real: P4's whole-generation reader
  stall, every generate and delete waiting on every in-flight search, about 600 lines of protocol,
  and ten-plus timing tests.
* **What replaces its non-1224 property:** O3's counter, not the gate. It keeps "no cached
  superseded mapping after invalidation" without any wait. It does not bound an in-flight search's
  pin; that bound is the search's own lifetime, as on unix since the project began. That is exactly
  what the MANDATE's first branch accepts.
* **Global counter versus per-path:** global, because a per-path map is a registry that needs a
  retention policy (`async-resource-invariants.md`). The cost of a global counter is an occasional
  uncached load.
* **Fold `invalidate_entries` into `invalidate_database`:** one caller remains, so a pass-through
  would be left. The R5 pin moves with it in the same commit.

## Decided autonomously

1. **Question:** Is the in-process search-index mapping gate (d-20260918-17) kept now that Windows
   replaces and unlinks a mapped sidecar? **Chosen:** remove it. Readers keep the generation they
   mapped on every platform. Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache before and after
   their single mutation. A process-wide invalidation counter makes `insert_index` skip caching a
   mapping opened before any invalidation that began after its snapshot. **Rejected:** keep the gate
   for disk-pin bounding (it blocks readers for a whole generation and writers for every search, to
   bound a pin that only an in-flight search or a late insert creates); remove it with no
   replacement (a late insert would pin a superseded or deleted generation until LRU, the next
   invalidation or exit — worse than today, P8); a per-path epoch registry (unbounded registry).
   **Reason:** `d-20260919-10`'s measurements remove the premise. The counter preserves the one
   non-1224 property without waits. Governs f-20260919-06; supersedes d-20260918-17's mechanism.
   **Reversal:** evidence of a supported volume or Windows build where an in-process mapping makes
   the POSIX-semantics replace or unlink fail.

* Non-goal: result-cache (`results`) freshness. It pins no disk and is keyed by provenance, so a late

## Approach

### O1 — The blocking gate is removed

MANDATE: "remove the gate (readers keep the generation they mapped, as on unix)"; "a cancellable
wait to every generate and delete and a lease to every load".

* Every P1/P2 gate symbol, its test hooks and the `DashMap` import that only it uses are deleted.
  `MmapSearchIndex` carries no lease, and the preferred load maps with `open_file_cancellable`.
* `generate_search_index_locked` and `delete_database_blocking` do not wait for readers. The single
  mutation (`write_entries_to_at`, `unlink_database_files`) runs exactly once, with no retry loop,
  at its current position. Deletion stays inside `delete_exclusive_cancellable`, and generation
  does not take `generation_lock` (the loader holds it).
* `SearchCache::clear` evicts the result and index caches only (it keeps `d-20260926-08`'s "never
  touches the lock registries").
* `insert_index` keeps its atomic get-or-insert under the `indexes` mutex (an existing entry for the
  identity is returned and the new mapping dropped). Displaced and evicted values are dropped after
  that mutex is released.
* `invalidate_entries`'s gate parameter and pointer eviction go. Its body folds into
  `invalidate_database`, its only remaining caller, so one function carries the canonicalize reach.
  Its `#[expect]` reason names the new site
  (`SearchCache::invalidate_database database.canonicalize`), and the R5 baseline entry is re-pinned
  to that function name and its new hash, with the method multiset unchanged (`{ canonicalize: 1 }`).
* Failure semantics: the generate and delete errors are unchanged (`MappedFile` stays classified and
  propagated for external mappers on non-POSIX volumes, P9). `Error::Cancellation` from the
  removed waits disappears; cancellation inside the row stream and the SQLite scope is unchanged.

### O2 — Pre- and post-mutation invalidation (restores the pre-gate shape)

MANDATE: removal must not lose the gate's pre-mutation eviction (P5, P6).

* Generation and deletion call `search_cache.invalidate_database(target.path())` immediately before
  their single mutation, where `begin_preferred_replace` stands today, as before `8fc057bf`.
* Deletion's post-mutation `invalidate_database` runs on **every** exit path once the
  `delete_exclusive_cancellable` closure has started the unlink, the `Err` path included (P5).
  Generation's existing post-commit invalidation on its committed and durability-uncertain paths is
  unchanged.
* Verification: `:4292` and `:4351` stay green unchanged, plus one deletion test whose unlink fails
  after the sidecar was removed (an existing fault hook, e.g. `UNLINK_SIDECAR_AFTER_IDENTITY_PROBE_HOOK`
  or the primary-removal fault) and which asserts that a mapping inserted between the pre- and
  post-mutation invalidations is not cached afterwards.

### O3 — No cached mapping outlives an invalidation (non-blocking)

MANDATE: "or keep it for a property other than 1224 — for example bounding how long a superseded
generation stays pinned on disk". This is the one such property the gate actually provided (P8).

* `SearchCache` holds a monotonically increasing invalidation counter: one process-wide atomic, no
  per-path registry, so retention is constant. `invalidate_database` increments it **before** its
  `results` and `indexes` critical sections.
* The preferred loader reads the counter **before** it opens the preferred leaf, and that snapshot
  reaches `insert_index`.
* Under the `indexes` mutex, `insert_index` first returns an existing entry for the identity
  (unchanged dedup). Otherwise, if the counter differs from the snapshot, it returns the freshly
  mapped index **uncached**; otherwise it inserts.
* Correctness, stated once: an invalidation's increment happens-before its `indexes` eviction. A
  reader insert whose critical section precedes that eviction is evicted by it; one that follows it
  sees the changed counter and is skipped. So after any `invalidate_database` returns, the cache
  holds no mapping opened before that invalidation began. An in-flight search still holds its own
  clone until it returns (cancellable), exactly as on unix. That is the accepted bound: "readers
  keep the generation they mapped".
* An invalidation of another database also makes a concurrent load skip caching. The cost is a
  re-map on that database's next search, never a wrong result (P13: invalidations are rare).
* **Failure semantics after the map (I4).** `cache_loaded_index` builds the cache identity from
  pathnames (`SearchIndexIdentity::for_database`, `main.rs:189`) after the leaf is mapped. Without
  the gate, a delete can unlink the database between the map and that construction. When identity
  construction fails **and** the invalidation counter has moved since the reader's snapshot, the
  loader returns the existing typed `Error::Conflict("search index changed while loading")` (the
  loader's own wording at `search.rs:309–310`) instead of the raw I/O error. With an unmoved
  counter the error propagates unchanged. A search racing its database's deletion therefore fails
  typed, as it already does when the delete lands before the probe (`tombstone_conflict`,
  `repository.rs:609/623`). Returning the mapping uncached without an identity is rejected because
  every caller keys its result cache by that identity (`search.rs:668/675`, `:919/926`).
* Verification goes through the **production loader** (I2). A test-only seam fires after the
  preferred leaf is mapped and before control leaves `open_valid_preferred`. Every test drives
  `load_search_index_cancellable`:
  (a) The seam calls `invalidate_database` for the same database. The loader returns a usable
  index (first entry readable) and `cached_index_count() == 0`. This goes red if the snapshot is
  taken anywhere after the leaf open.
  (b) The seam does nothing. The index is cached (control).
  (c) The seam invalidates a *different* database. The index is not cached (global counter,
  documented).
  (d) The seam unlinks the database file and invalidates. The loader returns `Error::Conflict` with
  the loader's wording, not `Error::Io`.

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Remove versus keep:** removed. The gate's reason is disproven on every POSIX-capable volume
  (P9). On the other volumes nothing mutates at all. Its cost is real: P4's whole-generation reader
  stall, every generate and delete waiting on every in-flight search, about 600 lines of protocol,
  and ten-plus timing tests.
* **What replaces its non-1224 property:** O3's counter, not the gate. It keeps "no cached
  superseded mapping after invalidation" without any wait. It does not bound an in-flight search's
  pin; that bound is the search's own lifetime, as on unix since the project began. That is exactly
  what the MANDATE's first branch accepts.
* **Global counter versus per-path:** global, because a per-path map is a registry that needs a
  retention policy (`async-resource-invariants.md`). The cost of a global counter is an occasional
  uncached load.
* **Fold `invalidate_entries` into `invalidate_database`:** one caller remains, so a pass-through
  would be left. The R5 pin moves with it in the same commit.

## Decided autonomously

1. **Question:** Is the in-process search-index mapping gate (d-20260918-17) kept now that Windows
   replaces and unlinks a mapped sidecar? **Chosen:** remove it. Readers keep the generation they
   mapped on every platform. Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache before and after
   their single mutation. A process-wide invalidation counter makes `insert_index` skip caching a
   mapping opened before any invalidation that began after its snapshot. A cache-identity
   construction failure after the map, with a moved counter, is returned as the loader's typed
   `Conflict("search index changed while loading")`. **Rejected:** keep the gate
   for disk-pin bounding (it blocks readers for a whole generation and writers for every search, to
   bound a pin that only an in-flight search or a late insert creates); remove it with no
   replacement (a late insert would pin a superseded or deleted generation until LRU, the next
   invalidation or exit — worse than today, P8); a per-path epoch registry (unbounded registry).
   **Reason:** `d-20260919-10`'s measurements remove the premise. The counter preserves the one
   non-1224 property without waits. Governs f-20260919-06; supersedes d-20260918-17's mechanism.
   **Reversal:** evidence of a supported volume or Windows build where an in-process mapping makes
   the POSIX-semantics replace or unlink fail.

## Approach

### O1 — The blocking gate is removed

MANDATE: "remove the gate (readers keep the generation they mapped, as on unix)"; "a cancellable
wait to every generate and delete and a lease to every load".

* Every P1/P2 gate symbol, its test hooks and the `DashMap` import that only it uses are deleted.
  `MmapSearchIndex` carries no lease, and the preferred load maps with `open_file_cancellable`.
* `generate_search_index_locked` and `delete_database_blocking` do not wait for readers. The single
  mutation (`write_entries_to_at`, `unlink_database_files`) runs exactly once, with no retry loop,
  at its current position. Deletion stays inside `delete_exclusive_cancellable`, and generation
  does not take `generation_lock` (the loader holds it).
* `SearchCache::clear` evicts the result and index caches only (it keeps `d-20260926-08`'s "never
  touches the lock registries").
* `insert_index` keeps its atomic get-or-insert under the `indexes` mutex (an existing entry for the
  identity is returned and the new mapping dropped). Displaced and evicted values are dropped after
  that mutex is released.
* `invalidate_entries`'s gate parameter and pointer eviction go. Its body folds into
  `invalidate_database`, its only remaining caller, so one function carries the canonicalize reach.
  Its `#[expect]` reason names the new site
  (`SearchCache::invalidate_database database.canonicalize`), and the R5 baseline entry is re-pinned
  to that function name and its new hash, with the method multiset unchanged (`{ canonicalize: 1 }`).
* Failure semantics: the generate and delete errors are unchanged (`MappedFile` stays classified and
  propagated for external mappers on non-POSIX volumes, P9). `Error::Cancellation` from the
  removed waits disappears; cancellation inside the row stream and the SQLite scope is unchanged.

### O2 — Pre- and post-mutation invalidation (restores the pre-gate shape)

MANDATE: removal must not lose the gate's pre-mutation eviction (P5, P6).

* Generation and deletion call `search_cache.invalidate_database(target.path())` immediately before
  their single mutation, where `begin_preferred_replace` stands today, as before `8fc057bf`.
* Deletion's post-mutation `invalidate_database` runs on **every** exit path once the
  `delete_exclusive_cancellable` closure has started the unlink, the `Err` path included (P5).
  Generation's existing post-commit invalidation on its committed and durability-uncertain paths is
  unchanged.
* Verification: `:4292` and `:4351` stay green unchanged, plus one deletion test whose unlink fails
  after the sidecar was removed (an existing fault hook, e.g. `UNLINK_SIDECAR_AFTER_IDENTITY_PROBE_HOOK`
  or the primary-removal fault) and which asserts that a mapping inserted between the pre- and
  post-mutation invalidations is not cached afterwards.

### O3 — No cached mapping outlives an invalidation (non-blocking)

MANDATE: "or keep it for a property other than 1224 — for example bounding how long a superseded
generation stays pinned on disk". This is the one such property the gate actually provided (P8).

* `SearchCache` holds a monotonically increasing invalidation counter: one process-wide atomic, no
  per-path registry, so retention is constant. `invalidate_database` increments it **before** its
  `results` and `indexes` critical sections.
* Each loader attempt reads the counter **before its database identity probe**, i.e. the
  `database_identity_expected` call whose `expected_source` that attempt's `open_valid_preferred`
  validates against (`search.rs:217`, `:241`, `:300`; the promotion re-open at `:271` reuses the
  `:241` probe and therefore its snapshot). That snapshot reaches `insert_index`. The promotion
  attempt's own `invalidate_database` (`search.rs:262`) follows that snapshot, so a just-promoted
  index is returned uncached once. Promotion happens at most once per legacy sidecar and the next
  search caches it, so this is accepted rather than re-snapshotted (a re-snapshot after `:262` would
  reopen the I4 window for that attempt). Reading it before
  the probe rather than before the leaf open is what makes the I4 failure contract below hold
  (I4 r2).
* Under the `indexes` mutex, `insert_index` first returns an existing entry for the identity
  (unchanged dedup). Otherwise, if the counter differs from the snapshot, it returns the freshly
  mapped index **uncached**; otherwise it inserts.
* Correctness, stated once: an invalidation's increment happens-before its `indexes` eviction. A
  reader insert whose critical section precedes that eviction is evicted by it; one that follows it
  sees the changed counter and is skipped. So after any `invalidate_database` returns, the cache
  holds no mapping opened before that invalidation began. An in-flight search still holds its own
  clone until it returns (cancellable), exactly as on unix. That is the accepted bound: "readers
  keep the generation they mapped".
* An invalidation of another database also makes a concurrent load skip caching. The cost is a
  re-map on that database's next search, never a wrong result (P13: invalidations are rare).
* **Failure semantics after the map (I4).** `cache_loaded_index` builds the cache identity from
  pathnames (`SearchIndexIdentity::for_database`, `main.rs:189`) after the leaf is mapped. Without
  the gate, a delete can unlink the database between the map and that construction. When identity
  construction fails **and** the invalidation counter has moved since the attempt's snapshot, the
  loader returns the existing typed `Error::Conflict("search index changed while loading")` (the
  loader's own wording at `search.rs:309–310`) instead of the raw I/O error. With an unmoved
  counter the error propagates unchanged. Every in-process delete ordering ends typed (P14):
  deletion reserves its tombstone (`repository.rs:837`, `:859`) before its closure runs the
  pre-mutation invalidation and then the unlink, and the read probe checks tombstones after probing
  (`repository.rs:623`). So either the reader's probe sees the tombstone and fails with
  `Conflict("database is being deleted")`, or the probe's check preceded the tombstone. In the
  second case the snapshot (taken before the probe) precedes the pre-mutation increment, which
  precedes every unlink, so the counter has moved by the time identity construction can fail.
  Returning the mapping uncached without an identity is rejected because every caller keys its
  result cache by that identity (`search.rs:668/675`, `:919/926`).
* Verification goes through the **production loader** (I2). A test-only seam fires immediately
  after the fast-path attempt's database identity probe returns, before `open_valid_preferred`. A
  seam later than the probe would let a misplaced snapshot pass (I2 r2). Every test drives
  `load_search_index_cancellable` over a valid preferred sidecar:
  (a) The seam calls `invalidate_database` for the same database. The loader returns a usable
  index (first entry readable) and `cached_index_count() == 0`. This goes red if the snapshot is
  read anywhere after the probe.
  (b) The seam does nothing. The index is cached (control).
  (c) The seam invalidates a *different* database. The index is not cached (global counter,
  documented).
  (d) The seam invalidates the same database and unlinks the **database file only** (the sidecar
  stays, so the descriptor-relative open and map succeed). The loader returns `Error::Conflict` with
  the loader's wording, not `Error::Io`. This is the reviewers' interleaving, with the
  pre-mutation increment after the probe and identity construction failing on the gone database.
  (e) The seam unlinks the database file without invalidating. The loader returns the unchanged
  `Error::Io` (control: only a moved counter converts).

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Remove versus keep:** removed. The gate's reason is disproven on every POSIX-capable volume
  (P9). On the other volumes nothing mutates at all. Its cost is real: P4's whole-generation reader
  stall, every generate and delete waiting on every in-flight search, about 600 lines of protocol,
  and ten-plus timing tests.
* **What replaces its non-1224 property:** O3's counter, not the gate. It keeps "no cached
  superseded mapping after invalidation" without any wait. It does not bound an in-flight search's
  pin; that bound is the search's own lifetime, as on unix since the project began. That is exactly
  what the MANDATE's first branch accepts.
* **Global counter versus per-path:** global, because a per-path map is a registry that needs a
  retention policy (`async-resource-invariants.md`). The cost of a global counter is an occasional
  uncached load.
* **Fold `invalidate_entries` into `invalidate_database`:** one caller remains, so a pass-through
  would be left. The R5 pin moves with it in the same commit.

## Decided autonomously

1. **Question:** Is the in-process search-index mapping gate (d-20260918-17) kept now that Windows
   replaces and unlinks a mapped sidecar? **Chosen:** remove it. Readers keep the generation they
   mapped on every platform. Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache before and after
   their single mutation. A process-wide invalidation counter makes `insert_index` skip caching a
   mapping opened before any invalidation that began after its snapshot. A cache-identity
   construction failure after the map, with a moved counter, is returned as the loader's typed

   invalidation or exit — worse than today, P8); a per-path epoch registry (unbounded registry).
   **Reason:** `d-20260919-10`'s measurements remove the premise. The counter preserves the one
   non-1224 property without waits. Governs f-20260919-06; supersedes d-20260918-17's mechanism.
   **Reversal:** evidence of a supported volume or Windows build where an in-process mapping makes
   the POSIX-semantics replace or unlink fail.

## Threat model and non-goals

Frozen at round 1.

* **T2 — external mappers.** Another process maps the sidecar. No lease ever saw these, and they are

* **Platforms:** Linux, macOS, Windows (NTFS through the POSIX-semantics `Ex` information classes).

  limit and stays outside this task.
* Non-goal: result-cache (`results`) freshness. It pins no disk and is keyed by provenance, so a late
  result insert is never served for another provenance (probe-1). It stays as it is.

## Approach

### O1 — The blocking gate is removed

MANDATE: "remove the gate (readers keep the generation they mapped, as on unix)"; "a cancellable
wait to every generate and delete and a lease to every load".

* Every P1/P2 gate symbol, its test hooks and the `DashMap` import that only it uses are deleted.
  `MmapSearchIndex` carries no lease, and the preferred load maps with `open_file_cancellable`.
* `generate_search_index_locked` and `delete_database_blocking` do not wait for readers. The single
  mutation (`write_entries_to_at`, `unlink_database_files`) runs exactly once, with no retry loop,
  at its current position. Deletion stays inside `delete_exclusive_cancellable`, and generation
  does not take `generation_lock` (the loader holds it).
* `SearchCache::clear` evicts the result and index caches only (it keeps `d-20260926-08`'s "never
  touches the lock registries").
* `insert_index` keeps its atomic get-or-insert under the `indexes` mutex (an existing entry for the
  identity is returned and the new mapping dropped). Displaced and evicted values are dropped after
  that mutex is released.
* `invalidate_entries`'s gate parameter and pointer eviction go. Its body folds into
  `invalidate_database`, its only remaining caller, so one function carries the canonicalize reach.
  Its `#[expect]` reason names the new site
  (`SearchCache::invalidate_database database.canonicalize`), and the R5 baseline entry is re-pinned
  to that function name and its new hash, with the method multiset unchanged (`{ canonicalize: 1 }`).
* Failure semantics: the generate and delete errors are unchanged (`MappedFile` stays classified and
  propagated for external mappers on non-POSIX volumes, P9). `Error::Cancellation` from the
  removed waits disappears; cancellation inside the row stream and the SQLite scope is unchanged.

### O2 — Pre- and post-mutation invalidation (restores the pre-gate shape)

MANDATE: removal must not lose the gate's pre-mutation eviction (P5, P6).

* Generation and deletion call `search_cache.invalidate_database(target.path())` immediately before
  their single mutation, where `begin_preferred_replace` stands today, as before `8fc057bf`.
* Deletion's post-mutation `invalidate_database` runs on **every** exit path once the
  `delete_exclusive_cancellable` closure has started the unlink, the `Err` path included (P5).
  Generation's existing post-commit invalidation on its committed and durability-uncertain paths is
  unchanged.
* Verification: `:4292` and `:4351` stay green unchanged, plus one deletion test whose unlink fails
  after the sidecar was removed (an existing fault hook, e.g. `UNLINK_SIDECAR_AFTER_IDENTITY_PROBE_HOOK`
  or the primary-removal fault) and which asserts that a mapping inserted between the pre- and
  post-mutation invalidations is not cached afterwards.

### O3 — No cached mapping outlives an invalidation (non-blocking)

MANDATE: "or keep it for a property other than 1224 — for example bounding how long a superseded
generation stays pinned on disk". This is the one such property the gate actually provided (P8).

* `SearchCache` holds a monotonically increasing invalidation counter: one process-wide atomic, no
  per-path registry, so retention is constant. `invalidate_database` increments it **before** its
  `results` and `indexes` critical sections.
* Each loader attempt reads the counter **before its database identity probe**, i.e. the
  `database_identity_expected` call whose `expected_source` that attempt's `open_valid_preferred`
  validates against (`search.rs:217`, `:241`, `:300`; the promotion re-open at `:271` reuses the
  `:241` probe and therefore its snapshot). That snapshot reaches `insert_index`. The promotion
  attempt's own `invalidate_database` (`search.rs:262`) follows that snapshot, so a just-promoted
  index is returned uncached once. Promotion happens at most once per legacy sidecar and the next
  search caches it, so this is accepted rather than re-snapshotted (a re-snapshot after `:262` would
  reopen the I4 window for that attempt). Reading it before
  the probe rather than before the leaf open is what makes the I4 failure contract below hold
  (I4 r2).
* Under the `indexes` mutex, `insert_index` first returns an existing entry for the identity
  (unchanged dedup). Otherwise, if the counter differs from the snapshot, it returns the freshly
  mapped index **uncached**; otherwise it inserts.
* Correctness, stated once: an invalidation's increment happens-before its `indexes` eviction. A
  reader insert whose critical section precedes that eviction is evicted by it; one that follows it
  sees the changed counter and is skipped. So after any `invalidate_database` returns, the cache
  holds no mapping opened before that invalidation began. An in-flight search still holds its own
  clone until it returns (cancellable), exactly as on unix. That is the accepted bound: "readers
  keep the generation they mapped".
* An invalidation of another database also makes a concurrent load skip caching. The cost is a
  re-map on that database's next search, never a wrong result (P13: invalidations are rare).
* **Failure semantics after the map (I4).** `cache_loaded_index` builds the cache identity from
  pathnames (`SearchIndexIdentity::for_database`, `main.rs:189`) after the leaf is mapped. Without
  the gate, a delete can unlink the database between the map and that construction. When identity
  construction fails **and** the invalidation counter has moved since the attempt's snapshot, the
  loader returns the existing typed `Error::Conflict("search index changed while loading")` (the
  loader's own wording at `search.rs:309–310`) instead of the raw I/O error. With an unmoved
  counter the error propagates unchanged. Every in-process delete ordering ends typed (P14):
  deletion reserves its tombstone (`repository.rs:837`, `:859`) before its closure runs the
  pre-mutation invalidation and then the unlink, and the read probe checks tombstones after probing
  (`repository.rs:623`). So either the reader's probe sees the tombstone and fails with
  `Conflict("database is being deleted")`, or the probe's check preceded the tombstone. In the
  second case the snapshot (taken before the probe) precedes the pre-mutation increment, which
  precedes every unlink, so the counter has moved by the time identity construction can fail.
  Returning the mapping uncached without an identity is rejected because every caller keys its
  result cache by that identity (`search.rs:668/675`, `:919/926`).
* Verification goes through the **production loader** (I2). A test-only seam fires immediately
  after the fast-path attempt's database identity probe returns, before `open_valid_preferred`. A
  seam later than the probe would let a misplaced snapshot pass (I2 r2). Every test drives
  `load_search_index_cancellable` over a valid preferred sidecar:
  (a) The seam calls `invalidate_database` for the same database. The loader returns a usable
  index (first entry readable) and `cached_index_count() == 0`. This goes red if the snapshot is
  read anywhere after the probe.
  (b) The seam does nothing. The index is cached (control).
  (c) The seam invalidates a *different* database. The index is not cached (global counter,
  documented).
  (d) The seam invalidates the same database and unlinks the **database file only** (the sidecar
  stays, so the descriptor-relative open and map succeed). The loader returns `Error::Conflict` with
  the loader's wording, not `Error::Io`. This is the reviewers' interleaving, with the
  pre-mutation increment after the probe and identity construction failing on the gone database.
  (e) The seam unlinks the database file without invalidating. The loader returns the unchanged
  `Error::Io` (control: only a moved counter converts).

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Remove versus keep:** removed. The gate's reason is disproven on every POSIX-capable volume
  (P9). On the other volumes nothing mutates at all. Its cost is real: P4's whole-generation reader
  stall, every generate and delete waiting on every in-flight search, about 600 lines of protocol,
  and ten-plus timing tests.
* **What replaces its non-1224 property:** O3's counter, not the gate. It keeps "no cached
  superseded mapping after invalidation" without any wait. It does not bound an in-flight search's
  pin; that bound is the search's own lifetime, as on unix since the project began. That is exactly
  what the MANDATE's first branch accepts.
* **Global counter versus per-path:** global, because a per-path map is a registry that needs a
  retention policy (`async-resource-invariants.md`). The cost of a global counter is an occasional
  uncached load.
* **Fold `invalidate_entries` into `invalidate_database`:** one caller remains, so a pass-through
  would be left. The R5 pin moves with it in the same commit.

## Decided autonomously

1. **Question:** Is the in-process search-index mapping gate (d-20260918-17) kept now that Windows
   replaces and unlinks a mapped sidecar? **Chosen:** remove it. Readers keep the generation they
   mapped on every platform. Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache before and after
   their single mutation. A process-wide invalidation counter makes `insert_index` skip caching a
   mapping opened before any invalidation that began after its snapshot. A cache-identity
   construction failure after the map, with a moved counter, is returned as the loader's typed
   `Conflict("search index changed while loading")`. **Rejected:** keep the gate
   for disk-pin bounding (it blocks readers for a whole generation and writers for every search, to
   bound a pin that only an in-flight search or a late insert creates); remove it with no
   replacement (a late insert would pin a superseded or deleted generation until LRU, the next
   invalidation or exit — worse than today, P8); a per-path epoch registry (unbounded registry).
   **Reason:** `d-20260919-10`'s measurements remove the premise. The counter preserves the one
   non-1224 property without waits. Governs f-20260919-06; supersedes d-20260918-17's mechanism.
   **Reversal:** evidence of a supported volume or Windows build where an in-process mapping makes
   the POSIX-semantics replace or unlink fail.

# Plan: retire the in-process search-index mapping gate (f-20260919-06)

## Goal

Answer f-20260919-06's open question and implement the answer. The gate's stated reason (Windows
refusing to replace or unlink a mapped `.ecsi` sidecar, 1224) has been measured false. Its blocking
protocol is removed. The one property it provided that is not about 1224 is kept, without waiting:
**no cached mapping outlives a search-cache invalidation of its database.** Readers keep the
generation they mapped, on every platform, as on unix.

Frozen at round 1.

## Approach

### O1 — The blocking gate is removed

MANDATE: "remove the gate (readers keep the generation they mapped, as on unix)"; "a cancellable
wait to every generate and delete and a lease to every load".

* Every P1/P2 gate symbol, its test hooks and the `DashMap` import that only it uses are deleted.
  `MmapSearchIndex` carries no lease, and the preferred load maps with `open_file_cancellable`.
* `generate_search_index_locked` and `delete_database_blocking` do not wait for readers. The single
  mutation (`write_entries_to_at`, `unlink_database_files`) runs exactly once, with no retry loop,
  at its current position. Deletion stays inside `delete_exclusive_cancellable`, and generation
  does not take `generation_lock` (the loader holds it).
* `SearchCache::clear` evicts the result and index caches only (it keeps `d-20260926-08`'s "never
  touches the lock registries").
* `insert_index` keeps its atomic get-or-insert under the `indexes` mutex (an existing entry for the
  identity is returned and the new mapping dropped). Displaced and evicted values are dropped after
  that mutex is released.
* `invalidate_entries`'s gate parameter and pointer eviction go. Its body folds into
  `invalidate_database`, its only remaining caller, so one function carries the canonicalize reach.
  Its `#[expect]` reason names the new site
  (`SearchCache::invalidate_database database.canonicalize`), and the R5 baseline entry is re-pinned
  to that function name and its new hash, with the method multiset unchanged (`{ canonicalize: 1 }`).
* Failure semantics: the generate and delete errors are unchanged (`MappedFile` stays classified and
  propagated for external mappers on non-POSIX volumes, P9). `Error::Cancellation` from the
  removed waits disappears; cancellation inside the row stream and the SQLite scope is unchanged.

### O2 — Pre- and post-mutation invalidation (restores the pre-gate shape)

MANDATE: removal must not lose the gate's pre-mutation eviction (P5, P6).

* Generation and deletion call `search_cache.invalidate_database(target.path())` immediately before
  their single mutation, where `begin_preferred_replace` stands today, as before `8fc057bf`.
* Deletion's post-mutation `invalidate_database` runs on **every** exit path once the
  `delete_exclusive_cancellable` closure has started the unlink, the `Err` path included (P5).
  Generation's existing post-commit invalidation on its committed and durability-uncertain paths is
  unchanged.
* Verification: `:4292` and `:4351` stay green unchanged, plus one deletion test whose unlink fails
  after the sidecar was removed (an existing fault hook, e.g. `UNLINK_SIDECAR_AFTER_IDENTITY_PROBE_HOOK`
  or the primary-removal fault) and which asserts that a mapping inserted between the pre- and
  post-mutation invalidations is not cached afterwards.

### O3 — No cached mapping outlives an invalidation (non-blocking)

MANDATE: "or keep it for a property other than 1224 — for example bounding how long a superseded
generation stays pinned on disk". This is the one such property the gate actually provided (P8).

* `SearchCache` holds a monotonically increasing invalidation counter: one process-wide atomic, no
  per-path registry, so retention is constant. `invalidate_database` increments it **before** its
  `results` and `indexes` critical sections.
* Each loader attempt reads the counter **before its database identity probe**, i.e. the
  `database_identity_expected` call whose `expected_source` that attempt's `open_valid_preferred`
  validates against (`search.rs:217`, `:241`, `:300`; the promotion re-open at `:271` reuses the
  `:241` probe and therefore its snapshot). That snapshot reaches `insert_index`. The promotion
  attempt's own `invalidate_database` (`search.rs:262`) follows that snapshot, so a just-promoted
  index is returned uncached once. Promotion happens at most once per legacy sidecar and the next
  search caches it, so this is accepted rather than re-snapshotted (a re-snapshot after `:262` would
  reopen the I4 window for that attempt). Reading it before
  the probe rather than before the leaf open is what makes the I4 failure contract below hold
  (I4 r2).
* Under the `indexes` mutex, `insert_index` first returns an existing entry for the identity
  (unchanged dedup). Otherwise, if the counter differs from the snapshot, it returns the freshly
  mapped index **uncached**; otherwise it inserts.
* Correctness, stated once: an invalidation's increment happens-before its `indexes` eviction. A
  reader insert whose critical section precedes that eviction is evicted by it; one that follows it
  sees the changed counter and is skipped. So after any `invalidate_database` returns, the cache
  holds no mapping opened before that invalidation began. An in-flight search still holds its own
  clone until it returns (cancellable), exactly as on unix. That is the accepted bound: "readers
  keep the generation they mapped".
* An invalidation of another database also makes a concurrent load skip caching. The cost is a
  re-map on that database's next search, never a wrong result (P13: invalidations are rare).
* **Failure semantics after the map (I4).** `cache_loaded_index` builds the cache identity from
  pathnames (`SearchIndexIdentity::for_database`, `main.rs:189`) after the leaf is mapped. Without
  the gate, a delete can unlink the database between the map and that construction. When identity
  construction fails **and** the invalidation counter has moved since the attempt's snapshot, the
  loader returns the existing typed `Error::Conflict("search index changed while loading")` (the
  loader's own wording at `search.rs:309–310`) instead of the raw I/O error. With an unmoved
  counter the error propagates unchanged. Every in-process delete ordering ends typed (P14):
  deletion reserves its tombstone (`repository.rs:837`, `:859`) before its closure runs the
  pre-mutation invalidation and then the unlink, and the read probe checks tombstones after probing
  (`repository.rs:623`). So either the reader's probe sees the tombstone and fails with
  `Conflict("database is being deleted")`, or the probe's check preceded the tombstone. In the
  second case the snapshot (taken before the probe) precedes the pre-mutation increment, which
  precedes every unlink, so the counter has moved by the time identity construction can fail.
  Returning the mapping uncached without an identity is rejected because every caller keys its
  result cache by that identity (`search.rs:668/675`, `:919/926`).
* Verification goes through the **production loader** (I2). A test-only seam fires immediately
  after the fast-path attempt's database identity probe returns, before `open_valid_preferred`. A
  seam later than the probe would let a misplaced snapshot pass (I2 r2). Every test drives
  `load_search_index_cancellable` over a valid preferred sidecar:
  (a) The seam calls `invalidate_database` for the same database. The loader returns a usable
  index (first entry readable) and `cached_index_count() == 0`. This goes red if the snapshot is
  read anywhere after the probe.
  (b) The seam does nothing. The index is cached (control).
  (c) The seam invalidates a *different* database. The index is not cached (global counter,
  documented).
  (d) The seam invalidates the same database and unlinks the **database file only** (the sidecar
  stays, so the descriptor-relative open and map succeed). The loader returns `Error::Conflict` with
  the loader's wording, not `Error::Io`. This is the reviewers' interleaving, with the
  pre-mutation increment after the probe and identity construction failing on the gone database.
  (e) The seam unlinks the database file without invalidating. The loader returns the unchanged
  `Error::Io` (control: only a moved counter converts).

## Decisions and trade-offs

* **Remove versus keep:** removed. The gate's reason is disproven on every POSIX-capable volume
  (P9). On the other volumes nothing mutates at all. Its cost is real: P4's whole-generation reader
  stall, every generate and delete waiting on every in-flight search, about 600 lines of protocol,
  and ten-plus timing tests.
* **What replaces its non-1224 property:** O3's counter, not the gate. It keeps "no cached
  superseded mapping after invalidation" without any wait. It does not bound an in-flight search's
  pin; that bound is the search's own lifetime, as on unix since the project began. That is exactly
  what the MANDATE's first branch accepts.
* **Global counter versus per-path:** global, because a per-path map is a registry that needs a
  retention policy (`async-resource-invariants.md`). The cost of a global counter is an occasional
  uncached load.
* **Fold `invalidate_entries` into `invalidate_database`:** one caller remains, so a pass-through
  would be left. The R5 pin moves with it in the same commit.

## Decided autonomously

1. **Question:** Is the in-process search-index mapping gate (d-20260918-17) kept now that Windows
   replaces and unlinks a mapped sidecar? **Chosen:** remove it. Readers keep the generation they
   mapped on every platform. Generation and deletion invalidate the search cache before and after
   their single mutation. A process-wide invalidation counter makes `insert_index` skip caching a
   mapping opened before any invalidation that began after its snapshot. A cache-identity
   construction failure after the map, with a moved counter, is returned as the loader's typed
   `Conflict("search index changed while loading")`. **Rejected:** keep the gate
   for disk-pin bounding (it blocks readers for a whole generation and writers for every search, to
   bound a pin that only an in-flight search or a late insert creates); remove it with no
   replacement (a late insert would pin a superseded or deleted generation until LRU, the next
   invalidation or exit — worse than today, P8); a per-path epoch registry (unbounded registry).
   **Reason:** `d-20260919-10`'s measurements remove the premise. The counter preserves the one
   non-1224 property without waits. Governs f-20260919-06; supersedes d-20260918-17's mechanism.
   **Reversal:** evidence of a supported volume or Windows build where an in-process mapping makes
   the POSIX-semantics replace or unlink fail.
