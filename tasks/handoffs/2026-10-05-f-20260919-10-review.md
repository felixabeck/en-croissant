## Mandate (fixed)

Close `f-20260919-10`: after a renderer reload an in-flight download shows its progress but
offers no Cancel, because the renderer job registry holding the ticket is emptied while the
native download continues. The same reload orphans other completion-owned native operations held
by renderer handles (analysis operation ids in tab stores). Open question: does a reload cancel
the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or does the renderer re-acquire
cancellation authority by a native lookup keyed on a display identity?

## Planned answer (after r3)

- Split by consumer. Accepted downloads survive a reload, and the renderer re-acquires Cancel
  through `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)`, which is owner-checked by `window.label()`
  and keeps the exact boolean of `d-20260920-02`. Each accepted download records its progress id
  at claim time. At most one uncancelled accepted download may exist per `(owner, progress id)`,
  and a duplicate claim is refused before a lease exists.
- `clear_progress` keeps its signature and meaning (the stale floor). While a live, uncancelled
  download's `Running` item holds the id, it clears nothing and returns that item's generation.
  A refused duplicate claim and a cancelled pre-claim attempt therefore never erase or hide the
  surviving bar.
- `cancelDownloadJob` falls back to the lookup when no local job exists. All four download cards
  offer Cancel whenever the bar runs. `ProgressButton` fences the generation it displayed when
  Cancel was pressed, so a late acknowledgement cannot hide a retry.
- `release_previous_document_operations()` is the first awaited step of `applicationStartup`. It
  cancels the owner's reservations (analyses with the supervised-engine signal shared with
  `cancel_analysis` and the destroy path, reads, unclaimed download tickets) before the new
  document prepares any reservation of its own; a failure is logged and never blocks startup.
- `verify:app` asserts the reload log line for a pre-reload reservation, and the destroyed-window
  line for a reservation made after the reload.
- Withdrawn: the r1 native `on_page_load` hook. Its ordering against the new document's first
  IPC could not be established. Also withdrawn: the r2 `clear_progress` refusal with `Conflict`,
  which made a cancelled pre-claim attempt hide the surviving bar.
- Deferred (filed through the inbox spool, ids allocated at merge):
  `20261005-032356-1787246-1791163436708290636-4.md` (the post-download renderer step lost on
  reload) and `20261005-033340-2110358-1791164020535061382-4.md` (I6, the failure surface of a
  surviving download).

### plan-r1.md

# Plan: a renderer reload keeps download Cancel and releases the previous document's native reservations

## Goal

After a renderer reload, an in-flight download still shows a working Cancel. The native
reservations that only the previous document could have consumed (analysis reports,
transient reads and unclaimed download tickets) are cancelled when the new document commits,
so none of them keeps running.

## MANDATE

Verbatim `f-20260919-10` (`tasks/findings.md`, filed 2026-09-20):

* **Where:** `src/utils/downloadJobs.ts` (module-level job registry introduced by `f-20260906-07`), `src/hooks/useProgress.ts` (`getProgress` restores the bar after reload), `src-tauri/src/infra/operations.rs` (accepted downloads are completion-owned; `cancel_owner` runs only on webview destroy, `src-tauri/src/main.rs:2562`).
* **Defect:** `f-20260906-07` makes download cancellation go through a renderer-held job registry keyed by progress id, holding the native-minted ticket. A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R, a crash-recovery reload) empties that registry while the native download keeps running under its accepted lease; `useProgress` restores the running bar from `get_progress`, and the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered. The user again cannot stop the transfer until it finishes. The same reload also orphans the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held in tab stores), so the class is broader than downloads.
* **Why it matters:** the mandate of `f-20260906-07` (a user can stop a download) does not survive a reload; low frequency in a desktop app, but the bar then shows a job the UI cannot act on.
* **Fix shape:** either native lookup — accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local job — or treat a reload like a destroy for completion-owned work (cancel the owner's accepted operations on page load of a known label). The second changes semantics for every accepted operation and needs its own design.
* **Open question:** should a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or should the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority for them by a native lookup keyed on a display identity?

(The registry file now lives at `src/hooks/downloadJobs.ts`; the destroy dispatch is in the
`app.run` closure of `src-tauri/src/main.rs`.)

## Threat model and non-goals

* **Inputs are accidental, not adversarial.** The renderer is the application's own code; a
  reload is a user keystroke (Ctrl+R) or WebKit's crash recovery. The authority boundary stays
  the one the codebase already enforces: the native **owner** of an operation is the webview
  label (`window.label()`), and no command may act on another owner's operation. A renderer that
  sends a wrong progress id can at worst cancel one of its own owner's downloads, which it could
  already do with the ticket.
* **Progress ids remain display identities** (`d-20260920-01`'s rejected alternative,
  `d-20260920-03`). They select among the caller's own downloads. They never grant authority
  across owners, and they never replace the ticket on the path where the renderer still holds it.
* **Environments:** the shipped Tauri app on Linux (WebKitGTK), macOS (WKWebView) and Windows
  (WebView2). Page-load semantics are measured on Linux through `pnpm verify:app` and traced in
  the wry source for the other two (see Traced premises). The macOS and Windows CI runners compile
  and run the unit tests only.

## Approach

### A. Native display identity and owner-checked lookup cancel

Rests on: "accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a
`cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local
job".

* **Recorded identity.** Each accepted download records the progress id it reports under, set at
  claim time by every one of the four download commands (P6): the renderer-supplied `id` for
  `download_file` and `download_engine_archive`, and the natively derived `lichess_{player}` /
  `chesscom_{player}` for the account flows, computed before the claim and identical to what
  `begin_progress` later receives.
* **Uniqueness.** At most one *uncancelled* accepted download per `(owner, progress id)`. A claim
  that would create a second one is refused with `Error::Conflict` before any lease exists. The
  ticket stays reserved and is released by the existing `withDownloadTicket` rejection path. A
  download whose token is already cancelled (winding down) does not block a retry: it publishes
  nothing (`d-20260920-02`), and its progress producer is superseded by the retry's `start` (P12).
* **Lookup cancel.** A new registry operation and command `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)`
  (owner = `window.label()`) has the exact boolean contract of `cancel_download`
  (`d-20260920-02`): `true` means that download will publish nothing; `false` means no
  uncancelled download of this owner is recorded under that id, or the matching one is already
  committing, so its own result stands. Among matching entries it acts on the uncancelled one.
  If only already-cancelled ones match, it answers `true` without side effects. Another owner's
  download under the same id is invisible to the lookup: it answers `false`, never `Conflict`,
  and never touches the other owner's entry.
* **Wiring.** The command is registered in the Specta command list beside `cancel_download`,
  `src/bindings/generated.ts` is regenerated (`pnpm bindings:generate`, never hand-edited), and
  owner threading is pinned by source-scan tests in the existing `main.rs`/`fs.rs` style
  (`d-20260920-04`).

### B. Renderer: Cancel available whenever a download bar is running

Depends on A. Rests on: "the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered" and "serves a
card with no local job".

* `cancelDownloadJob(progressId, errorTitle?)` keeps its local-entry path unchanged. With no local
  entry, it calls `cancelDownloadForProgress(progressId)` through the platform facade:
  * `true` → resolves `{ clearedGeneration: null }`, so `ProgressButton` fences the displayed
    generation. A cancelled terminal item renders no bar (`d-20260920-03`), and no native clear
    is issued: no local job exists whose retry a clear must be ordered against.
  * `false` → rejects with `downloadCancelError("lost")`. The running UI stays and the bar
    finishes on its own.
  * an IPC failure → notifies once under `errorTitle` and rejects with
    `downloadCancelError("request", error)`, exactly like the ticket path.
* All four download cards (P2) pass a cancel handler whenever they would show the running bar,
  not only while a local job is registered. `ProgressButton`'s `showProgress && onCancel` remains
  the only visibility rule. `UpgradeEngineModal` keeps its `cancel` (with `cancelIntent`) for the
  upgrade this document started and its `committing` suppression; for a card with no active
  upgrade in this document, it uses the lookup path.
* No change to `useProgress`, `runDownloadJob`, or the cards' `inProgress` derivation.

### C. A new document cancels the previous document's reservations

Independent of A and B (file-disjoint except `main.rs` registration, see Phases). Rests on: "The
same reload also orphans the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation
(analysis operation ids held in tab stores)" and the open question.

* **Answer to the open question, per class** (recorded as an autonomous decision):
  * **Accepted downloads survive a reload.** Their effect is completed natively (database and
    puzzle files are published by the command itself), their bar is deliberately restored
    (P3), and A+B give back the Cancel. Cancelling them on reload would silently discard a
    transfer the UI keeps showing.
  * **Every reservation of the reloading owner is cancelled when the new document commits**:
    analyses, transient reads and unclaimed download tickets. Their only consumer was the
    previous document's JavaScript. For analyses, the store already declares that consumer gone
    (P8).
* **Mechanism.** A `Builder::on_page_load` hook (P11) reacts to `PageLoadEvent::Started` for the
  webview's own label. It runs one registry operation, "cancel this owner's reservations", which
  `cancel_owner` is refactored to share: `cancel_owner` = that operation plus the owner's accepted
  downloads. The registry removes unclaimed reservations and cancels active ones exactly as
  `cancel_entry` does today. Accepted downloads and owner-less accepted operations are untouched.
  The first page load after startup finds nothing and does nothing. When `AppState` is not managed
  yet, the hook does nothing (`try_state`), because no reservation can exist before it is.
* **Analyses stop like a user cancel.** For each cancelled analysis id, both lifecycle paths
  (reload and destroy) send the same supervised-engine signal `cancel_analysis` sends (P10),
  through one shared helper that all three callers use.
* **Observability.** When the reload sweep cancelled anything, it logs one info line,
  `reloaded webview <label> cancelled native reservations: <comma-joined ids>`, beside the existing
  `destroyed webview …` line. The text is the contract that Phase 3 asserts. A registry failure
  logs at error level, like the destroy path, and never panics in the hook.
* **Ordering.** The sweep runs synchronously inside the commit callback, before any script of the
  new document executes (P11), so no reservation of the new document can predate it. Phase 3
  measures this on Linux.

### D. Real-window proof of the page-load semantics

Depends on C. Rests on: "A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R …)". `scripts/verify-app.mjs` extends
its existing retained-reservation pattern (P13):

* Prepare reservation T1, perform an in-app route navigation, and assert that no `reloaded webview`
  line names T1 and that T1 can still be claimed or cancelled (it was not swept).
* Prepare reservation T2, reload the document, wait for the application to become ready again,
  and assert the exact log line `reloaded webview main cancelled native reservations:` containing
  T2.
* The existing destroyed-window assertion keeps running after the reload, against a reservation
  prepared in the reloaded document.

This is growth of an existing verifier by one scenario of an existing shape (rule 6d disclosure:
the cheap alternative, a one-off scratch run, would leave the platform claim in P11 without a
regression check, and that claim is the whole of obligation C on Linux).

## Decisions and trade-offs

## Decided autonomously

* **Q1 — Does a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or
  does the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority?** Chosen: split by consumer. Accepted
  downloads survive and the renderer re-acquires Cancel through the owner-checked lookup by
  progress id (A, B). Every reservation of the reloading owner (analysis, read, unclaimed
  download ticket) is cancelled natively when the new document commits (C). Rejected: (a) reload
  cancels downloads too, the finding's second fix shape. It discards a transfer whose bar the app
  deliberately restores and whose effect is completed natively for databases and puzzles.
  (b) Persist tickets in `sessionStorage` and reuse `cancel_download`: renderer state is not
  authoritative for native jobs (`async-resource-invariants.md`), and stale tickets would need
  their own reconciliation. (c) A renderer-startup "sweep" command: an extra IPC and binding,
  plus an ordering obligation against every module-scope and startup request that the native
  commit hook does not need. (d) Cancel the persisted report `operationId` at tab rehydrate:
  background tabs rehydrate only when opened (P9), so their analyses keep running, and it covers
  analysis only. Reversal path: if a reload must also stop downloads, add accepted downloads to
  the page-load sweep (one call); A and B stay valid.
* **Q2 — How is the lookup made unambiguous?** Chosen: at most one uncancelled accepted download
  per `(owner, progress id)`, refused at claim; cancelled ones do not block a retry. Rejected:
  cancel every match (blurs `d-20260920-02`'s exact boolean); newest-claim-wins (keeps two live
  transfers under one bar). Reversal path: drop the claim check and define a multi-match answer.
* **Q3 — Do the lifecycle paths stop an analysis's engine, or only its token?** Chosen: the same
  supervised-engine signal as `cancel_analysis`, through one helper used by all three callers
  (rule 11: the second caller of "cancel an analysis" arrives in this change). Rejected:
  token-only, which leaves the engine searching the current move after its consumer is gone.
  Reversal path: drop the helper call from the two lifecycle paths.

## Risks / open questions

* R1 WebView2's `ContentLoading` timing relative to the previous document's last IPC is not
  measured (no Windows host). This is covered by the threat model's named limit.
* R2 An `on_page_load` hook that panics would run inside the platform callback. The hook must not
  `unwrap` and must log a registry error.
* R3 The reload scenario in `verify:app` waits for the application to become ready after a reload.
  If the existing readiness helper assumes a fresh process, the scenario needs its own wait on the
  same readiness signal, not a sleep.

### plan-r2.md

# Plan: a renderer reload keeps download Cancel and releases the previous document's native reservations

## Goal

After a renderer reload, an in-flight download still shows a working Cancel. The native
reservations that only the previous document could have consumed (analysis reports,
transient reads and unclaimed download tickets) are cancelled by the new document's first startup
step, so none of them keeps running.

## MANDATE

Verbatim `f-20260919-10` (`tasks/findings.md`, filed 2026-09-20):

* **Where:** `src/utils/downloadJobs.ts` (module-level job registry introduced by `f-20260906-07`), `src/hooks/useProgress.ts` (`getProgress` restores the bar after reload), `src-tauri/src/infra/operations.rs` (accepted downloads are completion-owned; `cancel_owner` runs only on webview destroy, `src-tauri/src/main.rs:2562`).
* **Defect:** `f-20260906-07` makes download cancellation go through a renderer-held job registry keyed by progress id, holding the native-minted ticket. A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R, a crash-recovery reload) empties that registry while the native download keeps running under its accepted lease; `useProgress` restores the running bar from `get_progress`, and the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered. The user again cannot stop the transfer until it finishes. The same reload also orphans the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held in tab stores), so the class is broader than downloads.
* **Why it matters:** the mandate of `f-20260906-07` (a user can stop a download) does not survive a reload; low frequency in a desktop app, but the bar then shows a job the UI cannot act on.
* **Fix shape:** either native lookup — accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local job — or treat a reload like a destroy for completion-owned work (cancel the owner's accepted operations on page load of a known label). The second changes semantics for every accepted operation and needs its own design.
* **Open question:** should a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or should the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority for them by a native lookup keyed on a display identity?

(The registry file now lives at `src/hooks/downloadJobs.ts`; the destroy dispatch is in the
`app.run` closure of `src-tauri/src/main.rs`.)

## Threat model and non-goals

* **Inputs are accidental, not adversarial.** The renderer is the application's own code; a
  reload is a user keystroke (Ctrl+R) or WebKit's crash recovery. The authority boundary stays
  the one the codebase already enforces: the native **owner** of an operation is the webview
  label (`window.label()`), and no command may act on another owner's operation. A renderer that
  sends a wrong progress id can at worst cancel one of its own owner's downloads, which it could
  already do with the ticket.
* **Progress ids remain display identities** (`d-20260920-01`'s rejected alternative,
  `d-20260920-03`). They select among the caller's own downloads. They never grant authority
  across owners, and they never replace the ticket on the path where the renderer still holds it.
* **Environments:** the shipped Tauri app on Linux (WebKitGTK), macOS (WKWebView) and Windows
  (WebView2). Page-load semantics are measured on Linux through `pnpm verify:app` and traced in
  the wry source for the other two (see Traced premises). The macOS and Windows CI runners compile
  and run the unit tests only.
* **Not defended:** an IPC request that the previous document sent before the navigation but that
  natively reaches its reservation step only after the new document committed. That window is
  bounded by in-flight IPC and recorded as a named limit, not a new mechanism.

## Approach

### A. Native display identity and owner-checked lookup cancel

Rests on: "accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a
`cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local
job".

* **Recorded identity.** Each accepted download records the progress id it reports under, set at
  claim time by every one of the four download commands (P6): the renderer-supplied `id` for
  `download_file` and `download_engine_archive`, and the natively derived `lichess_{player}` /
  `chesscom_{player}` for the account flows, computed before the claim and identical to what
  `begin_progress` later receives.
* **Uniqueness.** At most one *uncancelled* accepted download per `(owner, progress id)`. A claim
  that would create a second one is refused with `Error::Conflict` before any lease exists. The
  ticket stays reserved and is released by the existing `withDownloadTicket` rejection path. A
  download whose token is already cancelled (winding down) does not block a retry: it publishes
  nothing (`d-20260920-02`), and its progress producer is superseded by the retry's `start` (P12).
* **Lookup cancel.** A new registry operation and command `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)`
  (owner = `window.label()`) has the exact boolean contract of `cancel_download`
  (`d-20260920-02`): `true` means that download will publish nothing; `false` means no
  uncancelled download of this owner is recorded under that id, or the matching one is already
  committing, so its own result stands. Among matching entries it acts on the uncancelled one.
  If only already-cancelled ones match, it answers `true` without side effects. Another owner's
  download under the same id is invisible to the lookup: it answers `false`, never `Conflict`,
  and never touches the other owner's entry.
* **A running download's bar cannot be cleared by the renderer** (I3). `clear_progress(id)`
  refuses with `Error::Conflict`, without touching the store or emitting a cleared event, while any
  accepted download whose token is not cancelled is recorded under `id`. This keeps the existing
  `runDownloadJob` failure path (`downloadJobs.ts:84-85`, which clears on every failure) from
  erasing the surviving download's bar when its own claim was refused by the uniqueness rule. That
  failure path already treats a refused clear as `null` (`clearDownloadProgress`). Every legitimate
  clear is unaffected: a cancelled job's download is cancelled before its clear runs, and a failed
  or finished job's lease is dropped before its command answers (`fs.rs` `download_file` returns
  after `download_to_destination`; `run_native_operation` drops the lease before sending the
  result, `operations.rs:799-801`).
* **Wiring.** The command is registered in the Specta command list beside `cancel_download`,
  `src/bindings/generated.ts` is regenerated (`pnpm bindings:generate`, never hand-edited), and
  owner threading is pinned by source-scan tests in the existing `main.rs`/`fs.rs` style
  (`d-20260920-04`).

### B. Renderer: Cancel available whenever a download bar is running

Depends on A. Rests on: "the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered" and "serves a
card with no local job".

* `cancelDownloadJob(progressId, errorTitle?)` keeps its local-entry path unchanged. With no local
  entry, it calls `cancelDownloadForProgress(progressId)` through the platform facade:
  * `true` → resolves `{ clearedGeneration: null }`. A cancelled terminal item renders no bar
    (`d-20260920-03`), and no native clear is issued: no local job exists whose retry a clear must
    be ordered against.
  * `false` → rejects with `downloadCancelError("lost")`. The running UI stays and the bar
    finishes on its own.
  * an IPC failure → notifies once under `errorTitle` and rejects with
    `downloadCancelError("request", error)`, exactly like the ticket path.
* All four download cards (P2) pass a cancel handler whenever they would show the running bar,
  not only while a local job is registered. `ProgressButton`'s `showProgress && onCancel` remains
  the only visibility rule. `UpgradeEngineModal` keeps its `cancel` (with `cancelIntent`) for the
  upgrade this document started and its `committing` suppression; for a card with no active
  upgrade in this document, it uses the lookup path.
* **An acknowledgement hides only what was cancelled** (I2). `ProgressButton` records the
  generation it displays at the moment Cancel is pressed. When the acknowledgement carries
  `clearedGeneration: null` (`clearOnCancel={false}`), it fences that recorded generation (floor =
  recorded + 1) instead of `fence(null)`, which reads whatever is displayed when the late answer
  arrives (`useProgress.ts:92-97`). A retry started before a delayed acknowledgement, or a second
  Cancel's later answer, can no longer hide a newer generation. With nothing displayed at press
  time it keeps today's `fence(null)`. A non-null `clearedGeneration` and the `clearOnCancel`
  path are unchanged.
* No change to `useProgress`, `runDownloadJob`, or the cards' `inProgress` derivation.

### C. A new document cancels the previous document's reservations

Independent of A and B except shared files (see Phases). Rests on: "The same reload also orphans
the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held
in tab stores)" and the open question.

* **Answer to the open question, per class** (recorded as an autonomous decision):
  * **Accepted downloads survive a reload.** Their effect is completed natively (database and
    puzzle files are published by the command itself), their bar is deliberately restored
    (P3), and A+B give back the Cancel. Cancelling them on reload would silently discard a
    transfer the UI keeps showing.
  * **Every reservation of the reloading owner that exists when the new document starts is
    cancelled**: analyses, transient reads and unclaimed download tickets. Their only consumer was
    the previous document's JavaScript. For analyses, the store already declares that consumer
    gone (P8).
* **Mechanism: the new document asks for it before it does anything else** (I4 rewrite). A new
  command `release_previous_document_operations()` (owner = `window.label()`) runs one registry
  operation, "cancel this owner's reservations", which `cancel_owner` is refactored to share:
  `cancel_owner` = that operation plus the owner's accepted downloads. The registry removes
  unclaimed reservations and cancels active ones exactly as `cancel_entry` does today. Accepted
  downloads and owner-less accepted operations are untouched. On a first launch it finds nothing.
* **Ordering by construction.** `applicationStartup` (`src/index.tsx`, P14) awaits this command as
  its first step, before `initializePersistedSessions`, `initializeI18n` and the render of
  `<App />`. Every reservation this document prepares is issued after the command has answered,
  so no reservation of the new document can be swept. The guarantee rests on JavaScript order in
  one document, not on a webview callback's timing. If a module evaluated during import prepared a
  reservation at module scope, it would precede the sweep. Every reservation is prepared through
  `withPreparedTicket` (`src/platform/tauri.ts:245`, reached from `withDownloadTicket`,
  `invokeNativeRead` and `prepareAnalysis`), so the phase checks that no caller of those runs at
  module scope and records the result.
* **Failure semantics.** A rejected or failing call never blocks startup. The failure is logged
  through the existing startup diagnostic path (`logStartupDiagnostic` / `logFailureSafely`,
  `src/index.tsx:23-33`), and the application renders normally; the orphans then behave as they do
  today. A registry error inside the command is returned as a typed error, never a panic.
* **The native page-load hook (r1) is withdrawn.** Its ordering against the new document's first
  IPC rests on message order across WebKit's process boundary, which neither the wry mapping (P11)
  nor a `verify:app` scenario can establish (I4, three witnesses). P11 stays as the evidence for the
  withdrawal. Its closing sentence ("Phase 3 measures it on Linux") and the threat model's
  page-load measurement sentence no longer carry an obligation, because no obligation depends on
  page-load timing any more.
* **Analyses stop like a user cancel.** For each cancelled analysis id, both lifecycle paths
  (this command and the destroy path) send the same supervised-engine signal `cancel_analysis`
  sends (P10), through one shared helper that all three callers use.
* **Observability.** When the command cancelled anything, it logs one info line,
  `reloaded webview <label> cancelled native reservations: <comma-joined ids>`, beside the existing
  `destroyed webview …` line. The text is the contract that Phase 4 asserts.

### D. Real-window proof of a reload

Depends on C. Rests on: "A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R …)". `scripts/verify-app.mjs` extends
its existing retained-reservation pattern (P13):

* Prepare reservation T2, reload the document (WebDriver refresh or `location.reload()`), wait for
  the application's existing readiness signal, and assert the exact log line
  `reloaded webview main cancelled native reservations:` containing T2.
* The existing destroyed-window assertion then runs against a reservation prepared in the
  **reloaded** document. Its ticket appearing in the `destroyed webview` line proves that the
  reloaded document's own reservation survived the sweep until destroy.

This is growth of an existing verifier by one scenario of an existing shape (rule 6d disclosure:
the cheap alternative, a one-off scratch run, leaves no regression check that a real WebKitGTK
reload re-runs `applicationStartup` and reaches the sweep).

## Decisions and trade-offs

## Decided autonomously

* **Q1 — Does a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or
  does the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority?** Chosen: split by consumer. Accepted
  downloads survive and the renderer re-acquires Cancel through the owner-checked lookup by
  progress id (A, B). Every reservation of the reloading owner (analysis, read, unclaimed
  download ticket) is cancelled by the new document's first startup step,
  `release_previous_document_operations` (C). Rejected: (a) reload cancels downloads too, the
  finding's second fix shape. It discards a transfer whose bar the app deliberately restores and
  whose effect is completed natively for databases and puzzles. (b) Persist tickets in
  `sessionStorage` and reuse `cancel_download`: renderer state is not authoritative for native jobs
  (`async-resource-invariants.md`), and stale tickets would need their own reconciliation. (c) A
  native `on_page_load` hook (the r1 design): its ordering against the new document's first IPC
  depends on WebKit's cross-process message order, which cannot be established by the wry
  callback mapping or by `verify:app` (plan review r1, I4). (d) Cancel the persisted report
  `operationId` at tab rehydrate: background tabs rehydrate only when opened (P9), so their
  analyses keep running, and it covers analysis only. Reversal path: if a reload must also stop
  downloads, add accepted downloads to the startup sweep (one call); A and B stay valid.
* **Q2 — How is the lookup made unambiguous?** Chosen: at most one uncancelled accepted download
  per `(owner, progress id)`, refused at claim; cancelled ones do not block a retry. A running
  download's bar cannot be cleared through `clear_progress` (Q4). Rejected: cancel every match
  (blurs `d-20260920-02`'s exact boolean); newest-claim-wins (keeps two live transfers under one
  bar). Reversal path: drop the claim check and the clear guard together and define a multi-match
  answer.
* **Q3 — Do the lifecycle paths stop an analysis's engine, or only its token?** Chosen: the same
  supervised-engine signal as `cancel_analysis`, through one helper used by all three callers
  (rule 11: the second caller of "cancel an analysis" arrives in this change). Rejected:
  token-only, which leaves the engine searching the current move after its consumer is gone.
  Reversal path: drop the helper call from the two lifecycle paths.
* **Q4 — How does a refused duplicate claim avoid erasing the surviving download's bar?**
  Chosen: `clear_progress` refuses while an uncancelled accepted download records that progress
  id (A). Rejected: a dedicated typed "busy" error that `runDownloadJob` recognises, which adds an
  error variant and category mapping to the binding and still leaves any other clear caller able
  to erase a live bar; a renderer-side wait for the progress snapshot before enabling Install,
  which narrows the window but keeps it. Reversal path: remove the guard once no renderer path
  clears on a failure it did not start.
* **Q5 — How does a late cancel acknowledgement avoid hiding a retry?** Chosen: `ProgressButton`
  fences the generation it displayed when Cancel was pressed (B). Rejected: returning the progress
  generation from the native lookup, which needs the accepted entry to learn its progress lease
  generation after `begin_progress`, a second write into the registry per download. Reversal path:
  restore `fence(null)` at answer time.

## Risks / open questions

* R1 An IPC request the previous document sent before the reload, whose reservation natively lands
  after the new document's sweep, survives as an orphan. Bounded by in-flight IPC; named limit
  (threat model).
* R2 The reload scenario in `verify:app` waits for the application to become ready after a reload.
  If the existing readiness helper assumes a fresh process, the scenario needs its own wait on the
  same readiness signal, not a sleep.
* R3 Startup gains one awaited IPC before the first render. It is a single registry pass under the
  registry mutex, the same cost as the destroy path.

### plan-r3.md

# Plan: a renderer reload keeps download Cancel and releases the previous document's native reservations

## Goal

After a renderer reload, an in-flight download still shows a working Cancel. The native
reservations that only the previous document could have consumed (analysis reports,
transient reads and unclaimed download tickets) are cancelled by the new document's first startup
step, so none of them keeps running.

## MANDATE

Verbatim `f-20260919-10` (`tasks/findings.md`, filed 2026-09-20):

* **Where:** `src/utils/downloadJobs.ts` (module-level job registry introduced by `f-20260906-07`), `src/hooks/useProgress.ts` (`getProgress` restores the bar after reload), `src-tauri/src/infra/operations.rs` (accepted downloads are completion-owned; `cancel_owner` runs only on webview destroy, `src-tauri/src/main.rs:2562`).
* **Defect:** `f-20260906-07` makes download cancellation go through a renderer-held job registry keyed by progress id, holding the native-minted ticket. A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R, a crash-recovery reload) empties that registry while the native download keeps running under its accepted lease; `useProgress` restores the running bar from `get_progress`, and the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered. The user again cannot stop the transfer until it finishes. The same reload also orphans the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held in tab stores), so the class is broader than downloads.
* **Why it matters:** the mandate of `f-20260906-07` (a user can stop a download) does not survive a reload; low frequency in a desktop app, but the bar then shows a job the UI cannot act on.
* **Fix shape:** either native lookup — accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local job — or treat a reload like a destroy for completion-owned work (cancel the owner's accepted operations on page load of a known label). The second changes semantics for every accepted operation and needs its own design.
* **Open question:** should a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or should the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority for them by a native lookup keyed on a display identity?

(The registry file now lives at `src/hooks/downloadJobs.ts`; the destroy dispatch is in the
`app.run` closure of `src-tauri/src/main.rs`.)

## Threat model and non-goals

* **Inputs are accidental, not adversarial.** The renderer is the application's own code; a
  reload is a user keystroke (Ctrl+R) or WebKit's crash recovery. The authority boundary stays
  the one the codebase already enforces: the native **owner** of an operation is the webview
  label (`window.label()`), and no command may act on another owner's operation. A renderer that
  sends a wrong progress id can at worst cancel one of its own owner's downloads, which it could
  already do with the ticket.
* **Progress ids remain display identities** (`d-20260920-01`'s rejected alternative,
  `d-20260920-03`). They select among the caller's own downloads. They never grant authority
  across owners, and they never replace the ticket on the path where the renderer still holds it.
* **Environments:** the shipped Tauri app on Linux (WebKitGTK), macOS (WKWebView) and Windows
  (WebView2). Page-load semantics are measured on Linux through `pnpm verify:app` and traced in
  the wry source for the other two (see Traced premises). The macOS and Windows CI runners compile
  and run the unit tests only.
* **Not defended:** an IPC request that the previous document sent before the navigation but that
  natively reaches its reservation step only after the new document committed. That window is
  bounded by in-flight IPC and recorded as a named limit, not a new mechanism.

## Approach

### A. Native display identity and owner-checked lookup cancel

Rests on: "accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a
`cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local
job".

* **Recorded identity.** Each accepted download records the progress id it reports under, set at
  claim time by every one of the four download commands (P6): the renderer-supplied `id` for
  `download_file` and `download_engine_archive`, and the natively derived `lichess_{player}` /
  `chesscom_{player}` for the account flows, computed before the claim and identical to what
  `begin_progress` later receives.
* **Uniqueness.** At most one *uncancelled* accepted download per `(owner, progress id)`. A claim
  that would create a second one is refused with `Error::Conflict` before any lease exists. The
  ticket stays reserved and is released by the existing `withDownloadTicket` rejection path. A
  download whose token is already cancelled (winding down) does not block a retry: it publishes
  nothing (`d-20260920-02`), and its progress producer is superseded by the retry's `start` (P12).
* **Lookup cancel.** A new registry operation and command `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)`
  (owner = `window.label()`) has the exact boolean contract of `cancel_download`
  (`d-20260920-02`): `true` means that download will publish nothing; `false` means no
  uncancelled download of this owner is recorded under that id, or the matching one is already
  committing, so its own result stands. Among matching entries it acts on the uncancelled one.
  If only already-cancelled ones match, it answers `true` without side effects. Another owner's
  download under the same id is invisible to the lookup: it answers `false`, never `Conflict`,
  and never touches the other owner's entry.
* **A running download's bar cannot be cleared by the renderer** (I3, residual corrected in r3).
  `clear_progress(id)` keeps its signature and its meaning, "the generation floor below which this
  id's display is stale". When an accepted download whose token is not cancelled records `id` and
  the stored item for `id` is `Running`, the call clears nothing, emits nothing, and returns that
  item's generation. That item and its later updates stay above the floor. In every other case
  (no live download under `id`, or the stored item is terminal or absent) it clears exactly as
  today. Two consequences, both through the unchanged renderer code:
  * a claim refused by the uniqueness rule makes `runDownloadJob` clear (`downloadJobs.ts:84-85`);
    it now gets the surviving bar's generation back, so nothing is erased;
  * a local attempt cancelled **before its claim** while a surviving download is displayed (reload,
    Install before the snapshot arrives, then Cancel) settles through the same clear and
    acknowledges `{ clearedGeneration: <surviving generation> }`. `ProgressButton` fences below it,
    so the surviving bar and its Cancel (B) stay, and a second Cancel reaches the lookup.
  Every legitimate clear is unaffected: a cancelled job's own download is cancelled before its
  clear runs, and a failed or finished job's lease is dropped before its command answers (`fs.rs`
  `download_file` returns after `download_to_destination`; `run_native_operation` drops the lease
  before sending the result, `operations.rs:799-801`).
* **Wiring.** The command is registered in the Specta command list beside `cancel_download`,
  `src/bindings/generated.ts` is regenerated (`pnpm bindings:generate`, never hand-edited), and
  owner threading is pinned by source-scan tests in the existing `main.rs`/`fs.rs` style
  (`d-20260920-04`).

### B. Renderer: Cancel available whenever a download bar is running

Depends on A. Rests on: "the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered" and "serves a
card with no local job".

* `cancelDownloadJob(progressId, errorTitle?)` keeps its local-entry path unchanged. With no local
  entry, it calls `cancelDownloadForProgress(progressId)` through the platform facade:
  * `true` → resolves `{ clearedGeneration: null }`. A cancelled terminal item renders no bar
    (`d-20260920-03`), and no native clear is issued: no local job exists whose retry a clear must
    be ordered against.
  * `false` → rejects with `downloadCancelError("lost")`. The running UI stays and the bar
    finishes on its own.
  * an IPC failure → notifies once under `errorTitle` and rejects with
    `downloadCancelError("request", error)`, exactly like the ticket path.
* All four download cards (P2) pass a cancel handler whenever they would show the running bar,
  not only while a local job is registered. `ProgressButton`'s `showProgress && onCancel` remains
  the only visibility rule. `UpgradeEngineModal` keeps its `cancel` (with `cancelIntent`) for the
  upgrade this document started and its `committing` suppression; for a card with no active
  upgrade in this document, it uses the lookup path.
* **An acknowledgement hides only what was cancelled** (I2). `ProgressButton` records the
  generation it displays at the moment Cancel is pressed. When the acknowledgement carries
  `clearedGeneration: null` (`clearOnCancel={false}`), it fences that recorded generation (floor =
  recorded + 1) instead of `fence(null)`, which reads whatever is displayed when the late answer
  arrives (`useProgress.ts:92-97`). A retry started before a delayed acknowledgement, or a second
  Cancel's later answer, can no longer hide a newer generation. With nothing displayed at press
  time it keeps today's `fence(null)`. A non-null `clearedGeneration` and the `clearOnCancel`
  path are unchanged.
* No change to `useProgress`, `runDownloadJob`, or the cards' `inProgress` derivation.

### C. A new document cancels the previous document's reservations

Independent of A and B except shared files (see Phases). Rests on: "The same reload also orphans
the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held
in tab stores)" and the open question.

* **Answer to the open question, per class** (recorded as an autonomous decision):
  * **Accepted downloads survive a reload.** Their effect is completed natively (database and
    puzzle files are published by the command itself), their bar is deliberately restored
    (P3), and A+B give back the Cancel. Cancelling them on reload would silently discard a
    transfer the UI keeps showing.
  * **Every reservation of the reloading owner that exists when the new document starts is
    cancelled**: analyses, transient reads and unclaimed download tickets. Their only consumer was
    the previous document's JavaScript. For analyses, the store already declares that consumer
    gone (P8).
* **Mechanism: the new document asks for it before it does anything else** (I4 rewrite). A new
  command `release_previous_document_operations()` (owner = `window.label()`) runs one registry
  operation, "cancel this owner's reservations", which `cancel_owner` is refactored to share:
  `cancel_owner` = that operation plus the owner's accepted downloads. The registry removes
  unclaimed reservations and cancels active ones exactly as `cancel_entry` does today. Accepted
  downloads and owner-less accepted operations are untouched. On a first launch it finds nothing.
* **Ordering by construction.** `applicationStartup` (`src/index.tsx`, P14) awaits this command as
  its first step, before `initializePersistedSessions`, `initializeI18n` and the render of
  `<App />`. Every reservation this document prepares is issued after the command has answered,
  so no reservation of the new document can be swept. The guarantee rests on JavaScript order in
  one document, not on a webview callback's timing. If a module evaluated during import prepared a
  reservation at module scope, it would precede the sweep. Every reservation is prepared through
  `withPreparedTicket` (`src/platform/tauri.ts:245`, reached from `withDownloadTicket`,
  `invokeNativeRead` and `prepareAnalysis`), so the phase checks that no caller of those runs at
  module scope and records the result.
* **Failure semantics.** A rejected or failing call never blocks startup. The failure is logged
  through the existing startup diagnostic path (`logStartupDiagnostic` / `logFailureSafely`,
  `src/index.tsx:23-33`), and the application renders normally; the orphans then behave as they do
  today. A registry error inside the command is returned as a typed error, never a panic.
* **The native page-load hook (r1) is withdrawn.** Its ordering against the new document's first
  IPC rests on message order across WebKit's process boundary, which neither the wry mapping (P11)
  nor a `verify:app` scenario can establish (I4, three witnesses). P11 stays as the evidence for the
  withdrawal. Its closing sentence ("Phase 3 measures it on Linux") and the threat model's
  page-load measurement sentence no longer carry an obligation, because no obligation depends on
  page-load timing any more.
* **Analyses stop like a user cancel.** For each cancelled analysis id, both lifecycle paths
  (this command and the destroy path) send the same supervised-engine signal `cancel_analysis`
  sends (P10), through one shared helper that all three callers use.
* **Observability.** When the command cancelled anything, it logs one info line,
  `reloaded webview <label> cancelled native reservations: <comma-joined ids>`, beside the existing
  `destroyed webview …` line. The text is the contract that Phase 4 asserts.

### D. Real-window proof of a reload

Depends on C. Rests on: "A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R …)". `scripts/verify-app.mjs` extends
its existing retained-reservation pattern (P13):

* Prepare reservation T2, reload the document (WebDriver refresh or `location.reload()`), wait for
  the application's existing readiness signal, and assert the exact log line
  `reloaded webview main cancelled native reservations:` containing T2.
* The existing destroyed-window assertion then runs against a reservation prepared in the
  **reloaded** document. Its ticket appearing in the `destroyed webview` line proves that the
  reloaded document's own reservation survived the sweep until destroy.

This is growth of an existing verifier by one scenario of an existing shape (rule 6d disclosure:
the cheap alternative, a one-off scratch run, leaves no regression check that a real WebKitGTK
reload re-runs `applicationStartup` and reaches the sweep).

## Decisions and trade-offs

## Decided autonomously

* **Q1 — Does a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or
  does the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority?** Chosen: split by consumer. Accepted
  downloads survive and the renderer re-acquires Cancel through the owner-checked lookup by
  progress id (A, B). Every reservation of the reloading owner (analysis, read, unclaimed
  download ticket) is cancelled by the new document's first startup step,
  `release_previous_document_operations` (C). Rejected: (a) reload cancels downloads too, the
  finding's second fix shape. It discards a transfer whose bar the app deliberately restores and
  whose effect is completed natively for databases and puzzles. (b) Persist tickets in
  `sessionStorage` and reuse `cancel_download`: renderer state is not authoritative for native jobs
  (`async-resource-invariants.md`), and stale tickets would need their own reconciliation. (c) A
  native `on_page_load` hook (the r1 design): its ordering against the new document's first IPC
  depends on WebKit's cross-process message order, which cannot be established by the wry
  callback mapping or by `verify:app` (plan review r1, I4). (d) Cancel the persisted report
  `operationId` at tab rehydrate: background tabs rehydrate only when opened (P9), so their
  analyses keep running, and it covers analysis only. Reversal path: if a reload must also stop
  downloads, add accepted downloads to the startup sweep (one call); A and B stay valid.
* **Q2 — How is the lookup made unambiguous?** Chosen: at most one uncancelled accepted download
  per `(owner, progress id)`, refused at claim; cancelled ones do not block a retry. A running
  download's bar cannot be cleared through `clear_progress` (Q4). Rejected: cancel every match
  (blurs `d-20260920-02`'s exact boolean); newest-claim-wins (keeps two live transfers under one
  bar). Reversal path: drop the claim check and the `clear_progress` live branch together and
  define a multi-match answer.
* **Q3 — Do the lifecycle paths stop an analysis's engine, or only its token?** Chosen: the same
  supervised-engine signal as `cancel_analysis`, through one helper used by all three callers
  (rule 11: the second caller of "cancel an analysis" arrives in this change). Rejected:
  token-only, which leaves the engine searching the current move after its consumer is gone.
  Reversal path: drop the helper call from the two lifecycle paths.
* **Q4 — How does a refused duplicate claim, or a cancelled pre-claim attempt, avoid erasing or
  hiding the surviving download's bar?** Chosen: `clear_progress` returns the live `Running` item's
  generation as the stale floor, without clearing, while an uncancelled accepted download records
  that id (A). Same return type, same meaning ("floor"), no binding change. Rejected: refusing with
  `Error::Conflict` (the r2 design). The refusal reaches `cancelDownloadJob` as
  `clearedGeneration: null`, and the button then hides the surviving generation (plan review r2,
  I2/I3 residual). Telling it apart from other failures would need message matching, which
  `f-20260830-08` removed. Also rejected: a typed "busy" error variant, which adds an error variant
  and category mapping to the binding for the same effect; a `ClearOutcome` enum (rejected in
  `d-20260920-03`, and not needed when the floor already expresses the answer); gating Install on
  the progress snapshot, which narrows the window but leaves a Running item that arrives late.
  Reversal path: remove the live-download branch from `clear_progress` once no renderer path
  clears on a failure it did not start.
* **Q5 — How does a late cancel acknowledgement avoid hiding a retry?** Chosen: `ProgressButton`
  fences the generation it displayed when Cancel was pressed (B). Rejected: returning the progress
  generation from the native lookup, which needs the accepted entry to learn its progress lease
  generation after `begin_progress`, a second write into the registry per download. Reversal path:
  restore `fence(null)` at answer time.

### plan-r4.md

# Plan: a renderer reload keeps download Cancel and releases the previous document's native reservations

## Goal

After a renderer reload, an in-flight download still shows a working Cancel. The native
reservations that only the previous document could have consumed (analysis reports,
transient reads and unclaimed download tickets) are cancelled by the new document's first startup
step, so none of them keeps running.

## MANDATE

Verbatim `f-20260919-10` (`tasks/findings.md`, filed 2026-09-20):

* **Where:** `src/utils/downloadJobs.ts` (module-level job registry introduced by `f-20260906-07`), `src/hooks/useProgress.ts` (`getProgress` restores the bar after reload), `src-tauri/src/infra/operations.rs` (accepted downloads are completion-owned; `cancel_owner` runs only on webview destroy, `src-tauri/src/main.rs:2562`).
* **Defect:** `f-20260906-07` makes download cancellation go through a renderer-held job registry keyed by progress id, holding the native-minted ticket. A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R, a crash-recovery reload) empties that registry while the native download keeps running under its accepted lease; `useProgress` restores the running bar from `get_progress`, and the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered. The user again cannot stop the transfer until it finishes. The same reload also orphans the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held in tab stores), so the class is broader than downloads.
* **Why it matters:** the mandate of `f-20260906-07` (a user can stop a download) does not survive a reload; low frequency in a desktop app, but the bar then shows a job the UI cannot act on.
* **Fix shape:** either native lookup — accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local job — or treat a reload like a destroy for completion-owned work (cancel the owner's accepted operations on page load of a known label). The second changes semantics for every accepted operation and needs its own design.
* **Open question:** should a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or should the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority for them by a native lookup keyed on a display identity?

(The registry file now lives at `src/hooks/downloadJobs.ts`; the destroy dispatch is in the
`app.run` closure of `src-tauri/src/main.rs`.)

## Threat model and non-goals

* **Inputs are accidental, not adversarial.** The renderer is the application's own code; a
  reload is a user keystroke (Ctrl+R) or WebKit's crash recovery. The authority boundary stays
  the one the codebase already enforces: the native **owner** of an operation is the webview
  label (`window.label()`), and no command may act on another owner's operation. A renderer that
  sends a wrong progress id can at worst cancel one of its own owner's downloads, which it could
  already do with the ticket.
* **Progress ids remain display identities** (`d-20260920-01`'s rejected alternative,
  `d-20260920-03`). They select among the caller's own downloads. They never grant authority
  across owners, and they never replace the ticket on the path where the renderer still holds it.

## Approach

### A. Native display identity and owner-checked lookup cancel

Rests on: "accepted downloads record their progress id and owner, and a
`cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)` command (owner-checked) serves a card with no local
job".

* **Recorded identity.** Each accepted download records the progress id it reports under, set at
  claim time by every one of the four download commands (P6): the renderer-supplied `id` for
  `download_file` and `download_engine_archive`, and the natively derived `lichess_{player}` /
  `chesscom_{player}` for the account flows, computed before the claim and identical to what
  `begin_progress` later receives.
* **Uniqueness.** At most one *uncancelled* accepted download per `(owner, progress id)`. A claim
  that would create a second one is refused with `Error::Conflict` before any lease exists. The
  ticket stays reserved and is released by the existing `withDownloadTicket` rejection path. A
  download whose token is already cancelled (winding down) does not block a retry: it publishes
  nothing (`d-20260920-02`), and its progress producer is superseded by the retry's `start` (P12).
* **Lookup cancel.** A new registry operation and command `cancel_download_for_progress(progress_id)`
  (owner = `window.label()`) has the exact boolean contract of `cancel_download`
  (`d-20260920-02`): `true` means that download will publish nothing; `false` means no
  uncancelled download of this owner is recorded under that id, or the matching one is already
  committing, so its own result stands. Among matching entries it acts on the uncancelled one.
  If only already-cancelled ones match, it answers `true` without side effects. Another owner's
  download under the same id is invisible to the lookup: it answers `false`, never `Conflict`,
  and never touches the other owner's entry.
* **A running download's bar cannot be cleared by the renderer** (I3, residual corrected in r3).
  `clear_progress(id)` keeps its signature and its meaning, "the generation floor below which this
  id's display is stale". When an accepted download whose token is not cancelled records `id` and
  the stored item for `id` is `Running`, the call clears nothing, emits nothing, and returns that
  item's generation. That item and its later updates stay above the floor. In every other case
  (no live download under `id`, or the stored item is terminal or absent) it clears exactly as
  today. Two consequences, both through the unchanged renderer code:
  * a claim refused by the uniqueness rule makes `runDownloadJob` clear (`downloadJobs.ts:84-85`);
    it now gets the surviving bar's generation back, so nothing is erased;
  * a local attempt cancelled **before its claim** while a surviving download is displayed (reload,
    Install before the snapshot arrives, then Cancel) settles through the same clear and
    acknowledges `{ clearedGeneration: <surviving generation> }`. `ProgressButton` fences below it,
    so the surviving bar and its Cancel (B) stay, and a second Cancel reaches the lookup.
  Every legitimate clear is unaffected: a cancelled job's own download is cancelled before its
  clear runs, and a failed or finished job's lease is dropped before its command answers (`fs.rs`
  `download_file` returns after `download_to_destination`; `run_native_operation` drops the lease
  before sending the result, `operations.rs:799-801`).
* **Wiring.** The command is registered in the Specta command list beside `cancel_download`,
  `src/bindings/generated.ts` is regenerated (`pnpm bindings:generate`, never hand-edited), and
  owner threading is pinned by source-scan tests in the existing `main.rs`/`fs.rs` style
  (`d-20260920-04`).

### B. Renderer: Cancel available whenever a download bar is running

Depends on A. Rests on: "the card renders no Cancel because no job is registered" and "serves a
card with no local job".

* `cancelDownloadJob(progressId, errorTitle?)` keeps its local-entry path unchanged. With no local
  entry, it calls `cancelDownloadForProgress(progressId)` through the platform facade:
  * `true` → resolves `{ clearedGeneration: null }`. A cancelled terminal item renders no bar
    (`d-20260920-03`), and no native clear is issued: no local job exists whose retry a clear must
    be ordered against.
  * `false` → rejects with `downloadCancelError("lost")`. The running UI stays and the bar
    finishes on its own.
  * an IPC failure → notifies once under `errorTitle` and rejects with
    `downloadCancelError("request", error)`, exactly like the ticket path.
* All four download cards (P2) pass a cancel handler whenever they would show the running bar,
  not only while a local job is registered. `ProgressButton`'s `showProgress && onCancel` remains
  the only visibility rule. `UpgradeEngineModal` keeps its `cancel` (with `cancelIntent`) for the
  upgrade this document started and its `committing` suppression; for a card with no active
  upgrade in this document, it uses the lookup path.
* **An acknowledgement hides only what was cancelled** (I2). `ProgressButton` records the
  generation it displays at the moment Cancel is pressed. When the acknowledgement carries
  `clearedGeneration: null` (`clearOnCancel={false}`), it fences that recorded generation (floor =
  recorded + 1) instead of `fence(null)`, which reads whatever is displayed when the late answer
  arrives (`useProgress.ts:92-97`). A retry started before a delayed acknowledgement, or a second
  Cancel's later answer, can no longer hide a newer generation. With nothing displayed at press
  time it keeps today's `fence(null)`. A non-null `clearedGeneration` and the `clearOnCancel`
  path are unchanged.
* No change to `useProgress`, `runDownloadJob`, or the cards' `inProgress` derivation.

### C. A new document cancels the previous document's reservations

Independent of A and B except shared files (see Phases). Rests on: "The same reload also orphans
the renderer handles of any other completion-owned native operation (analysis operation ids held
in tab stores)" and the open question.

* **Answer to the open question, per class** (recorded as an autonomous decision):
  * **Accepted downloads survive a reload.** Their effect is completed natively (database and
    puzzle files are published by the command itself), their bar is deliberately restored
    (P3), and A+B give back the Cancel. Cancelling them on reload would silently discard a
    transfer the UI keeps showing.
  * **Every reservation of the reloading owner that exists when the new document starts is
    cancelled**: analyses, transient reads and unclaimed download tickets. Their only consumer was
    the previous document's JavaScript. For analyses, the store already declares that consumer
    gone (P8).
* **Mechanism: the new document asks for it before it does anything else** (I4 rewrite). A new
  command `release_previous_document_operations()` (owner = `window.label()`) runs one registry
  operation, "cancel this owner's reservations", which `cancel_owner` is refactored to share:
  `cancel_owner` = that operation plus the owner's accepted downloads. The registry removes
  unclaimed reservations and cancels active ones exactly as `cancel_entry` does today. Accepted
  downloads and owner-less accepted operations are untouched. On a first launch it finds nothing.
* **Ordering by construction.** `applicationStartup` (`src/index.tsx`, P14) awaits this command as
  its first step, before `initializePersistedSessions`, `initializeI18n` and the render of
  `<App />`. Every reservation this document prepares is issued after the command has answered,
  so no reservation of the new document can be swept. The guarantee rests on JavaScript order in
  one document, not on a webview callback's timing. If a module evaluated during import prepared a
  reservation at module scope, it would precede the sweep. Every reservation is prepared through
  `withPreparedTicket` (`src/platform/tauri.ts:245`, reached from `withDownloadTicket`,
  `invokeNativeRead` and `prepareAnalysis`), so the phase checks that no caller of those runs at
  module scope and records the result.
* **Failure semantics.** A rejected or failing call never blocks startup. The failure is logged
  through the existing startup diagnostic path (`logStartupDiagnostic` / `logFailureSafely`,
  `src/index.tsx:23-33`), and the application renders normally; the orphans then behave as they do
  today. A registry error inside the command is returned as a typed error, never a panic.
* **The native page-load hook (r1) is withdrawn.** Its ordering against the new document's first
  IPC rests on message order across WebKit's process boundary, which neither the wry mapping (P11)
  nor a `verify:app` scenario can establish (I4, three witnesses). P11 stays as the evidence for the
  withdrawal. Its closing sentence ("Phase 3 measures it on Linux") and the threat model's
  page-load measurement sentence no longer carry an obligation, because no obligation depends on
  page-load timing any more.
* **Analyses stop like a user cancel.** For each cancelled analysis id, both lifecycle paths
  (this command and the destroy path) send the same supervised-engine signal `cancel_analysis`
  sends (P10), through one shared helper that all three callers use.
* **Observability.** When the command cancelled anything, it logs one info line,
  `reloaded webview <label> cancelled native reservations: <comma-joined ids>`, beside the existing
  `destroyed webview …` line. The text is the contract that Phase 4 asserts.

### D. Real-window proof of a reload

Depends on C. Rests on: "A webview reload (WebKitGTK Ctrl+R …)". `scripts/verify-app.mjs` extends
its existing retained-reservation pattern (P13):

* Prepare reservation T2, reload the document (WebDriver refresh or `location.reload()`), wait for
  the application's existing readiness signal, and assert the exact log line
  `reloaded webview main cancelled native reservations:` containing T2.
* The existing destroyed-window assertion then runs against a reservation prepared in the
  **reloaded** document. Its ticket appearing in the `destroyed webview` line proves that the
  reloaded document's own reservation survived the sweep until destroy.

This is growth of an existing verifier by one scenario of an existing shape (rule 6d disclosure:
the cheap alternative, a one-off scratch run, leaves no regression check that a real WebKitGTK
reload re-runs `applicationStartup` and reaches the sweep).

## Decisions and trade-offs

## Decided autonomously

* **Q1 — Does a renderer reload cancel the reloading webview's completion-owned operations, or
  does the renderer re-acquire cancellation authority?** Chosen: split by consumer. Accepted
  downloads survive and the renderer re-acquires Cancel through the owner-checked lookup by
  progress id (A, B). Every reservation of the reloading owner (analysis, read, unclaimed
  download ticket) is cancelled by the new document's first startup step,
  `release_previous_document_operations` (C). Rejected: (a) reload cancels downloads too, the
  finding's second fix shape. It discards a transfer whose bar the app deliberately restores and
  whose effect is completed natively for databases and puzzles. (b) Persist tickets in
  `sessionStorage` and reuse `cancel_download`: renderer state is not authoritative for native jobs
  (`async-resource-invariants.md`), and stale tickets would need their own reconciliation. (c) A
  native `on_page_load` hook (the r1 design): its ordering against the new document's first IPC
  depends on WebKit's cross-process message order, which cannot be established by the wry
  callback mapping or by `verify:app` (plan review r1, I4). (d) Cancel the persisted report
  `operationId` at tab rehydrate: background tabs rehydrate only when opened (P9), so their
  analyses keep running, and it covers analysis only. Reversal path: if a reload must also stop
  downloads, add accepted downloads to the startup sweep (one call); A and B stay valid.
* **Q2 — How is the lookup made unambiguous?** Chosen: at most one uncancelled accepted download
  per `(owner, progress id)`, refused at claim; cancelled ones do not block a retry. A running
  download's bar cannot be cleared through `clear_progress` (Q4). Rejected: cancel every match
  (blurs `d-20260920-02`'s exact boolean); newest-claim-wins (keeps two live transfers under one
  bar). Reversal path: drop the claim check and the `clear_progress` live branch together and
  define a multi-match answer.
* **Q3 — Do the lifecycle paths stop an analysis's engine, or only its token?** Chosen: the same
  supervised-engine signal as `cancel_analysis`, through one helper used by all three callers
  (rule 11: the second caller of "cancel an analysis" arrives in this change). Rejected:
  token-only, which leaves the engine searching the current move after its consumer is gone.
  Reversal path: drop the helper call from the two lifecycle paths.
* **Q4 — How does a refused duplicate claim, or a cancelled pre-claim attempt, avoid erasing or
  hiding the surviving download's bar?** Chosen: `clear_progress` returns the live `Running` item's
  generation as the stale floor, without clearing, while an uncancelled accepted download records
  that id (A). Same return type, same meaning ("floor"), no binding change. Rejected: refusing with
  `Error::Conflict` (the r2 design). The refusal reaches `cancelDownloadJob` as
  `clearedGeneration: null`, and the button then hides the surviving generation (plan review r2,
  I2/I3 residual). Telling it apart from other failures would need message matching, which
  `f-20260830-08` removed. Also rejected: a typed "busy" error variant, which adds an error variant
  and category mapping to the binding for the same effect; a `ClearOutcome` enum (rejected in
  `d-20260920-03`, and not needed when the floor already expresses the answer); gating Install on
  the progress snapshot, which narrows the window but leaves a Running item that arrives late.
  Reversal path: remove the live-download branch from `clear_progress` once no renderer path
  clears on a failure it did not start.
* **Q5 — How does a late cancel acknowledgement avoid hiding a retry?** Chosen: `ProgressButton`
  fences the generation it displayed when Cancel was pressed (B). Rejected: returning the progress
  generation from the native lookup, which needs the accepted entry to learn its progress lease
  generation after `begin_progress`, a second write into the registry per download. Reversal path:
  restore `fence(null)` at answer time.
