# Plan: `spawn_registered` hands its caller the armed guard of the published generation (f-20260914-35)

## Goal

No caller of `spawn_registered` ever holds a published engine generation without an owner that
terminates it when the caller's future is dropped. The configuration probe of `get_engine_config`
and the report-analysis engine of `analyze_game_core` stay owned by an armed `RegistrationGuard`
from publication until their own termination has completed, so dropping either future at any
await removes exactly that generation from `EngineSupervisor` instead of leaving it registered
until application exit.

## Approach

### O1 — `spawn_registered` returns the armed guard of the generation it published

MANDATE: "a drop guard held across the `spawn_registered` → `terminate_exact` span"; f-20260926-03:
"once `initialize_admitted_actor` disarms its `RegistrationGuard`, nothing owns the generation
except the future itself".

* On initialization success, `initialize_admitted_actor` no longer disarms its guard; the success
  value of `initialize_admitted_actor`, `spawn_registered` and the test-only
  `initialize_registered_actor` carries that armed `RegistrationGuard` alongside the
  `SupervisedEngine` and the initialization value. The failure path is unchanged.
* Ownership contract: from publication until the caller hands the generation to another owner
  (`disarm`) or until a termination of it has completed (`terminate_now`), exactly one armed guard
  owns the published generation, so no successful return of `spawn_registered` leaves the
  generation unowned at any await. Dropping the guard keeps today's `RegistrationGuard` drop
  semantics (P2): a spawned, generation-exact `terminate_exact` that logs its own failure, which
  leaves the generation registered — and therefore reachable by every supervisor drain — until it
  completes.
* No await is introduced between the guard's arming and the caller receiving it.

### O2 — `get_engine_config` terminates its probe through the guard

MANDATE: the finding's Defect and Open question.

* `get_engine_config` keeps the returned guard and terminates the probe with `terminate_now()`
  instead of calling `terminate_exact` on the supervisor. A drop of the command future while that
  termination is pending leaves the guard armed, and its drop terminates the probe (P2).
* Failure semantics unchanged for the renderer: a failed termination is still returned as the
  command's `Err` (today's `?` on `terminate_exact`), and `terminate_now` additionally logs it under
  key and generation, as `d-20260927-08` already has the actor do.
* The open question's alternative — a supervisor-owned probing operation — is not taken
  (`d-20260927-03` rejected an `OperationLease` wrapper; D1).

### O3 — `analyze_game_core` holds the guard until its engine's termination completed

MANDATE (f-20260926-03): "hold the published generation in a guard across the awaits and disarm
it at the existing `terminate_exact`".

* `EngineProcess::new` passes the guard through to `analyze_game_core`, which holds it across every
  await until the generation is terminated.
* Every termination of that generation in `analyze_game_core` — the success-path termination and

## Approach

### O1 — `spawn_registered` returns the armed guard of the generation it published

MANDATE: "a drop guard held across the `spawn_registered` → `terminate_exact` span"; f-20260926-03:
"once `initialize_admitted_actor` disarms its `RegistrationGuard`, nothing owns the generation
except the future itself".

* On initialization success, `initialize_admitted_actor` no longer disarms its guard; the success
  value of `initialize_admitted_actor`, `spawn_registered` and the test-only
  `initialize_registered_actor` carries that armed `RegistrationGuard` alongside the
  `SupervisedEngine` and the initialization value. The failure path is unchanged.
* Ownership contract: from publication until the caller hands the generation to another owner
  (`disarm`) or until a termination of it has completed (`terminate_now`), exactly one armed guard
  owns the published generation, so no successful return of `spawn_registered` leaves the
  generation unowned at any await. Dropping the guard keeps today's `RegistrationGuard` drop
  semantics (P2): a spawned, generation-exact `terminate_exact` that logs its own failure, which
  leaves the generation registered — and therefore reachable by every supervisor drain — until it
  completes.
* No await is introduced between the guard's arming and the caller receiving it.

### O2 — `get_engine_config` terminates its probe through the guard

MANDATE: the finding's Defect and Open question.

* `get_engine_config` keeps the returned guard and terminates the probe with `terminate_now()`
  instead of calling `terminate_exact` on the supervisor. A drop of the command future while that
  termination is pending leaves the guard armed, and its drop terminates the probe (P2).
* Failure semantics unchanged for the renderer: a failed termination is still returned as the
  command's `Err` (today's `?` on `terminate_exact`), and `terminate_now` additionally logs it under
  key and generation, as `d-20260927-08` already has the actor do.
* The open question's alternative — a supervisor-owned probing operation — is not taken
  (`d-20260927-03` rejected an `OperationLease` wrapper; D1).

### O3 — `analyze_game_core` holds the guard until its engine's termination completed

MANDATE (f-20260926-03): "hold the published generation in a guard across the awaits and disarm
it at the existing `terminate_exact`".

* `EngineProcess::new` passes the guard through to `analyze_game_core`, which holds it across every
  await until the generation is terminated.
* Every termination of that generation in `analyze_game_core` — the success-path termination and
  the one inside `finish_analysis_failure` — goes through the guard's `terminate_now()`, so a drop
  while either termination is pending still terminates it (P2).
* After the guard was consumed by a completed termination, a later failure (novelty phase) performs
  no second termination: the entry was already removed whether that termination succeeded or failed
  (P3), so today's repeated `terminate_exact` is a no-op and dropping it changes no result.
  Returned errors and terminal progress stay as today (cleanup `Ok` → the primary error; cleanup
  `Err` → today's `OperationAndCleanup` fold, unchanged).

### O4 — Game construction takes the spawned guard instead of re-creating one

MANDATE: "a drop guard held across the … span" — one owner per generation (`d-20260927-03`).

* The game spawn helpers hand the guard they receive to `start_game`, and
  `GameEngineConstruction::own` takes that guard instead of constructing a second one from the
  `RegisteredGameEngine` (P7). The ownership model of `d-20260927-03` (guards disarmed at
  `transfer_to_live_session`, terminated by `abandon`) is unchanged; only who creates the guard
  changes.
* Tests that call `own` with fake registered engines construct their guard explicitly.

## Approach

### O1 — `spawn_registered` returns the armed guard of the generation it published

MANDATE: "a drop guard held across the `spawn_registered` → `terminate_exact` span"; f-20260926-03:
"once `initialize_admitted_actor` disarms its `RegistrationGuard`, nothing owns the generation
except the future itself".

* On initialization success, `initialize_admitted_actor` no longer disarms its guard; the success
  value of `initialize_admitted_actor`, `spawn_registered` and the test-only
  `initialize_registered_actor` carries that armed `RegistrationGuard` alongside the
  `SupervisedEngine` and the initialization value. The failure path is unchanged.
* Ownership contract: from publication until the caller hands the generation to another owner
  (`disarm`) or until a termination of it has completed (`terminate_now`), exactly one armed guard
  owns the published generation, so no successful return of `spawn_registered` leaves the
  generation unowned at any await. Dropping the guard keeps today's `RegistrationGuard` drop
  semantics (P2): a spawned, generation-exact `terminate_exact` that logs its own failure, which
  leaves the generation registered — and therefore reachable by every supervisor drain — until it
  completes.
* No await is introduced between the guard's arming and the caller receiving it.
* Every consumer of the success value decides the guard's fate explicitly, test code included. The
  production callers do so per O2–O4. A `#[cfg(test)]` consumer that today discards or ignores the
  success value (P9) disarms the returned guard, which reproduces the base's success-path `disarm`
  exactly, so its existing assertions hold unchanged; in particular a publisher that survives a
  scoped pending drain stays registered with its actor never terminated. Only V1 and the new V2/V3
  cases let a returned guard drop or terminate.

### O2 — `get_engine_config` terminates its probe through the guard

MANDATE: the finding's Defect and Open question.

* `get_engine_config` keeps the returned guard and terminates the probe with `terminate_now()`
  instead of calling `terminate_exact` on the supervisor. A drop of the command future while that
  termination is pending leaves the guard armed, and its drop terminates the probe (P2).
* Failure semantics unchanged for the renderer: a failed termination is still returned as the
  command's `Err` (today's `?` on `terminate_exact`), and `terminate_now` additionally logs it under
  key and generation, as `d-20260927-08` already has the actor do.
* The open question's alternative — a supervisor-owned probing operation — is not taken
  (`d-20260927-03` rejected an `OperationLease` wrapper; D1).

### O3 — `analyze_game_core` holds the guard until its engine's termination completed

MANDATE (f-20260926-03): "hold the published generation in a guard across the awaits and disarm
it at the existing `terminate_exact`".

* `EngineProcess::new` passes the guard through to `analyze_game_core`, which holds it across every
  await until the generation is terminated.
* Every termination of that generation in `analyze_game_core` — the success-path termination and
  the one inside `finish_analysis_failure` — goes through the guard's `terminate_now()`, so a drop
  while either termination is pending still terminates it (P2).
* After the guard was consumed by a completed termination, a later failure (novelty phase) performs
  no second termination: the entry was already removed whether that termination succeeded or failed
  (P3), so today's repeated `terminate_exact` is a no-op and dropping it changes no result.
  Returned errors and terminal progress stay as today (cleanup `Ok` → the primary error; cleanup
  `Err` → today's `OperationAndCleanup` fold, unchanged).

### O4 — Game construction takes the spawned guard instead of re-creating one

MANDATE: "a drop guard held across the … span" — one owner per generation (`d-20260927-03`).

* The game spawn helpers hand the guard they receive to `start_game`, and
  `GameEngineConstruction::own` takes that guard instead of constructing a second one from the
  `RegisteredGameEngine` (P7). The ownership model of `d-20260927-03` (guards disarmed at
  `transfer_to_live_session`, terminated by `abandon`) is unchanged; only who creates the guard
  changes.
* Tests that call `own` with fake registered engines construct their guard explicitly.
